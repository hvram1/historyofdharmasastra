Chapter V. Names of All Prāyashchittas

Volume IV pp. 130–152

This chapter undertakes a systematic enumeration and elucidation of the diverse individual prāyaścittas. This involves not merely listing their specific nomenclature but also delving into the nuanced descriptions associated with each. The concept of prāyaścitta, central to Dharmaśāstra, serves as the mechanism for atonement and purification from various transgressions, thereby restoring an individual's spiritual standing within the framework of dharma. These names represent the specific terms and classifications for different acts of prāyaścitta – or expiation – prescribed for various categories of sins or misdeeds. Following this nomenclature, the descriptions provide a detailed account of each prāyaścitta. Such descriptions typically delineate the specific rituals, austerities, observances, or charitable acts that constitute the expiation. They often specify the duration, method of performance, the qualifications of the performer, and the precise circumstances under which a particular prāyaścitta is deemed appropriate and effective. The comprehensive nature of this survey aims to cover all individual forms of such atonement, as outlined across the vast corpus of Smṛti and Purāṇic literature.

— Synopsis by P.V. Kane, from History of Dharmaśāstra

IV. 5.18 and others define Cāndrāyana (of the Yavamadhya type) as follows: On the first day of the bright half of a month only one morsel of food (grāsa or piṇḍa) is taken, on the second tithi two and so on; on the full moon (Paurnamāsī) 15 morsels are taken; then on the first of the dark half 14 morsels, one being reduced on each succeeding day. So on the 14th of the dark half only one morsel is eaten and on the amāvasyā there is a complete fast. Here in the middle of the month of thirty days the largest number of morsels is taken (and so this is Yavamadhya) just as on the Paurnamāsī day the moon becomes full and begins to wane from the next day. Here Paurnamāsī is in the middle of the period of the observance as Viṣṇu Dh. This Prājāpatya has several varietieṣ The first is that described by Manu XI. 211 and Vaṣ 21. 20 viz. four periods of three days each following one another in which there is respectively eating once only by day, once only by night, eating once only without asking for it and complete fast.
