Chapter VI. Consequences of Not Undergoing Penances

Volume IV pp. 153–179

The profound consequences that arise from not undergoing prescribed expiations or `prāyaścitta` are meticulously examined. A historical account traces the evolving concepts of Heaven, Hells, and the various forms of torments described within the Dharmaśāstra tradition, illustrating the grim destinations awaiting those who fail to rectify their transgressions. Central to understanding these ramifications is the profound doctrine of `karmavipāka`, which meticulously details how the fruits of one's actions, both virtuous and unrighteous, mature and manifest, thereby determining one's future states and experiences. This intricate system of justice underscores the imperative of penance. Furthermore, the crucial concept of `Śuddhi`, or purification, is illuminated, exploring its multifaceted role, including the specific purificatory rites associated with events such as birth and death. These rites, alongside general expiations, are deemed essential for maintaining ritual purity, the neglect of which bears significant spiritual and social implications within the Hindu framework.

— Synopsis by P.V. Kane, from History of Dharmaśāstra

Chapter VI. Consequences of Not Undergoing Penances

If a sinner did not undergo a prāyaścitta, the Smṛtis, Purāṇas, medieval digests declared that he had to suffer dire and far-reaching consequenceṣ Yāj. III. 221 declares that men addicted to sins, who feel no repentance and who (therefore) do not undergo the proper penances, fall into painful and horrible hellṣ Manu XII. 54 ff and Yāj. III. 206 ff provide that those guilty of grave (and other) sins after having passed numbers of years in hells and suffered the tortures of horrible hells are born again in this world because of (the remnants of their) evil actions in different forms such as lower animals, insects, trees and shrubs &c. Manu XI. 53 ordains that one should always perform penance in order to free oneself from sins, since those who have not destroyed their sins (by means of prāyaścittas) are born again endowed with disgraceful marks (such as having bad nails, black teeth &c.). Manu XI. 48 also emphasizes that wicked men suffer mal-formations owing to their wicked deeds committed in this very life or in a former life. The Viṣṇu-purāṇa1 echoes the words of Yāj. III. 221. The Viṣṇudharmo-ttara declares2 that those (sinners) who do not undergo penances nor are punished by the king fall into hell and are born also as lower animals and even after attaining human bodies they become marked (with bodily defects). Viṣṇu Dh. S. (44. 1-10) prescribes3 that evil-doers, having experienced the horrors of hell pass into animal bodies and that those guilty of atipātakas, mahāpātakas, anupātakas, upapātakas, jāti- bhrāmsa-kara actions, sankarīkaraṇa, apatrakaraṇa, malini- karana and miscellaneous sins, respectively enter the bodies of all plants, of worms (or insects), of birds, of aquatic animals, of animals roving in waters, of deer, of cattle, of untouchables among men, of miscellaneous and cruel carnivorous animals (like tigers). Viṣṇu Dh. S. 45.1 provides4 that sinners, after having undergone the horrors of hells and after having passed through the bodies of lower animals, are born as human beings with certain marks (indicative of their sins). So the teaching of these and other authorities comes to this that sins when not expiated by penances or by State punishment, lead to hell, that then, owing to some remnants of the evil deeds, to birth as lower animals and then as decrepit or diseased human beingṣ
