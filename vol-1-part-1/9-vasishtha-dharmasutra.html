9. Vasishtha-Dharmasutra

Volume I: Part I pp. 94–112

The various editions of the Vāsiṣṭha-Dharmasūtra exhibit differing chapter counts. Kumārila’s assertion that this text was specially studied by Ṛgvedins prompts an examination, yet nothing inherently special within the Vāsiṣṭha-Dharmasūtra itself directly connects it to the Ṛgveda. Its style closely resembles that of Gautama, with many sūtras being identical to those found in both Gautama and Baudhāyana. The overall form of the Vāsiṣṭha-Dharmasūtra also bears a resemblance to the Baudhāyana-dharmasūtra. Medhātithi, Mitākṣarā, and Viśvarūpa frequently quote from almost all chapters of the extant Vāsiṣṭha, indicating its significant recognition. Vāsiṣṭha was aware of a broad range of literature and notably prohibits the learning of the language of Mlecchas, while also naming several authors on dharmaśāstra. Vāsiṣṭha's references to the views of Manu are made with respect to a work almost identical to the present Manusmṛti, and these do not necessitate the postulation of a separate Mānavadharmaśūtra. Bühler's interpretation of Vas. Dh. S. 4.8 as a quotation from a Mānavadharmaśūtra is therefore contestable. Only in Vas. Dh. S. 12.16 and 19.37, where Manu is quoted, do we find no corresponding verses in the extant Manusmṛti. Conversely, over forty verses are entirely common to Vāsiṣṭha and the present Manusmṛti, leading to the conclusion that Vāsiṣṭha contains borrowings from the present Manusmṛti or its prototype in verse. Vas. Dh. S. 22 is notably identical to Gautama 19, while Dr. Jolly's view that Vas. Dh. S. 28. 10-15 and 18-22 are borrowed from Viṣṇudharmasūtra or its original, the Kāṭhakadharmasūtra, appears incorrect. Bühler's speculation on Vāsiṣṭha’s home being north of the Narmadā lacks definitive evidence. The earliest reference to Vasiṣṭha as a writer on dharma appears in Manu 8.140, placing the age of Vas. Dh. S. between 300 and 100 B.C. Vāsiṣṭha’s views are ancient, particularly concerning secondary sons, the Dattaka son, niyoga, and remarriage. He enumerates only six forms of marriage, though his stance on matters such as adoption and documents differs from Gautama or Baudhāyana. The possibility that Vas. 18.4 (Rāmaka, or Romaka as a variant reading) contains a reference to the Romans has been considered. The author of the extant Vāsiṣṭha-Dharmasūtra appears to be eclectic, and the text itself seems to be a re-hash of the Āpastamba-dharmasūtra, Baudhāyana-dharmasūtra, and Manu. Passages quoted from or referred to in the Saṃhitās and Brāhmaṇas significantly outnumber those from the Ṛgveda, suggesting that Vāsiṣṭha-Dharmasūtra did not initially align itself with the Ṛgveda. Dr. Ram Gopal's remarks regarding the passages of Vāsiṣṭha and the three Gṛhya sūtras attached to the Ṛgveda are thus misleading. Other compilations include a Vṛddha-Vasiṣṭha, an early compilation, as well as a Bṛhad Vasiṣṭha and a Jyotir Vasiṣṭha. Yajñavāmin is known to have commented upon the Vāsiṣṭha-Dharmasūtra.

Kumārila (vide note 55 above) tells us that the dharmasūtra of Vasiṣṭha was specially studied by the students of the Ṛgveda, but that along with other dharmasūtras it is authoritative for all caraṇaṣ No śrauta and gṛhya sūtras of Vasiṣṭha, if they ever existed, have come down to uṣ We have therefore to fall back upon one of two hypotheses, viz. either the dharmasūtra of Vasiṣṭha is the solitary remnant of a school that might have once possessed a complete kalpa or that it was composed as an independent work on dharma and was subsequently seized upon by the students of the Ṛgveda, who had only śrauta and gṛhya sūtras of Āśvalāyana. For reasons given elsewhere I incline to the latter view. The dharmasūtra of Vasiṣṭha cites quotations from all Vedas and beyond the name Vasiṣṭha there is hardly anything special in the dharmasūtra to connect it with the Ṛgveda. It is true that in the 17th chap. several verses of the Ṛgveda (such as I. 21. 5, I. 124. 7, V. 4. 10 which occur in sūtras 3, 16 and 4 respectively ) and several passages from the story of Śunaḥśepa in the Aitareya-brāhmaṇa are quoted by the sūtra and that several hymns of the Ṛgveda, such as the asyavāmīya (Ṛg. I. 164 ), haviṣpāntiya (Ṛg. X. 88), Aghamarṣaṇa (X. 190), are referred to in the 26th chap. But there is nothing remarkable in this as some of the verses and sūktas are mentioned in the Baudhāyanadharmasūtra also. Besides, the Vaṣ Dh. S. quotes several passages from the Taittirīyasaṁhitā ( as in Vaṣ V. 7–9, which quote Tai. S. II. 5. 1-6 and Vaṣ XI. 48 which quotes Tai. S. The Smṛticandrikā quotes about 20 verses from Vṛddha-Vasiṣṭha on āhnika and śrāddha; Bhaṭṭoji in his gloss on the Caturviṁśatimata (p. 12) seems to quote a prose passage from Vṛddha-Vasiṣṭha. From the above it follows that Vṛddha-Vasiṣṭha was an early compilation and dealt with almost all such topics (including Vyavahāra) as are dealt with by Yāj. The Mit. also quotes on Yāj. III 287 a Bṛhad-Vasiṣṭha. The Smṛticandrikā (III. p. 300) quotes a few verses from a Jyotir-Vasiṣṭha. The I. O. catalogue (No. 1339 p. 392) speaks of a Vasiṣṭha-smṛti in ten *adhyāya*s about the religious observances and duties enjoined on devotees of Viṣṇu.
