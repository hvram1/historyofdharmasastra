78. The Kalpataru of Lakshmīdhara

Volume I: Part II pp. 663–699

The *Kalpataru* of Lakṣmīdhara stands as an extensive work, profoundly influencing early Mithilā and Bengal writers. This monumental text is meticulously divided into fourteen *kāṇḍas*, whose intricate arrangement and specific contents have been thoroughly documented. Notably, eleven of these *kāṇḍas* were judiciously edited by Prof. K. V. Rangaswami Aiyangar. The personal history of Lakṣmīdhara is also detailed, establishing the composition of the *Kalpataru* between 1125 and 1145 A.D., within the realm of Govindacandra. Regarding its textual relationships, the passages concerning *vyavahāra* in the *Kalpataru* exhibit striking resemblances, being more or less identical with those found in the *Mitākṣarā*. However, arguments advanced by Prof. Aiyangar suggesting that the *Mitākṣarā* knew or relied upon the *Kalpataru* are considered incorrect. The *Kalpataru* frequently interacts with foundational texts such as the *Manusmṛti* and *Yājñavalkyasmṛti*. While it is notable for its extensive size and wide scope, in terms of quality, the *Kalpataru* is generally regarded as inferior to the *Mitākṣarā* and several other prominent digests. Furthermore, specific passages from Devala, as cited in *Aparārka*, find parallels within the *Mokṣakāṇḍa* of the *Kalpataru*.

— Synopsis by P.V. Kane, from History of Dharmaśāstra

One remarkable circumstance bearing on the relative chronological positions of the Mitākṣarā and the Kalpataru may be noted here. The Hāralatā of Aniruddhabhaṭṭa was composed about 1160 to 1170 A. D., as he was the guru and Dharmādhyakṣa of king Ballālasena of Bengal, who composed his Dānasāgara in Śaka 1091 (1169-70 A. D.). Two works viz. Kāmadhenu and Kalpataru were composed in the realm of the Gāhaḍwāla King Govindacandra. The Hāralatā mentions the Kāmadhenu several times as an authority of the same weight as Viśvarūpa, Bhojadeva, and Govindarāja (vide pp. 41, 117, 174 of the Hāralatā); while the vast digest of Kalpataru of Lakṣmī- dhara is not mentioned even once anywhere by the Hāralatā. If the Kāmadhenu, disdainfully spoken of by Lakṣmīdhara, could penetrate to Bengal before 1160-70 A. D. and be treat- ed there as a work of authority, it is very surprising that the Kalpataru backed by the glamorous career of the great Minister Lakṣmīdhara and dealing at length with the same topics as those treated of in the Hāralatā should not have been referred to even once in the Hāralatā.

This leads to the conclusion that portions of the Kalpa- taru were composed decades later than the time when the Kāmadhenu was composed and earlier than 1160 A. D. only by a decade or so.
