Pulastya is established as an important expounder of dharma, a figure whose name is notably mentioned in a verse from Vṛddha Yājñavalkya. His influence is evident through the widespread citations found in the works of later, authoritative legal commentators.

For instance, Viśvarūpa, as well as the renowned authors of the Mitākṣarā and Aparārka, extensively quote numerous verses attributed to Pulastya. These particular citations delve into intricate details concerning daily observances, or *āhnika*, and the crucial ancestral rites known as *śrāddha*, underscoring his contribution to practical ritual law. Furthermore, the *Dānanīratnākara* cites a distinct prose passage identified as Pulastya's, suggesting a diverse textual legacy beyond mere versification.

Consequently, scholarly analysis places the period of composition for the works ascribed to Pulastya between the fourth and seventh centuries A.D., situating his contributions within a formative era of Dharmaśāstra literature.
