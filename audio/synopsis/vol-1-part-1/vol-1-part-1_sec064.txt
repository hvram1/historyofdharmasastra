Medhātithi is primarily known for his extensive commentary on Manu, a work that, in Dr. Ganganath Jha's edition, is noted to be missing ten verses in its third adhyāya. Further, the printed bhāṣya exhibits corruption in the eighth, ninth, and twelfth chapters. The historical reference to King Madana having restored Medhātithi's bhāṣya has been thoroughly explained in this context. While Dr. Jolly proposed Medhātithi was a southerner, this perspective is considered incorrect, with evidence suggesting he was a northerner, likely a Kashmirian.

Medhātithi's intellectual landscape reveals a profound knowledge of various smṛtis, and he notably quotes or mentions figures such as Asahāya, Bhartryajña, Yajvan, Upādhyāya, Rju, and Viṣṇusvāmin. He was deeply steeped in Pūrva-mīmāṁsā philosophy, a characteristic evident in his bhāṣya which frequently employs the terms vidhi and arthavāda. He also utilizes several nyāyas to interpret the Manuṣmṛti, and his references to Śarīraka, along with his relationship with Śaṅkarācārya, are subjects of scholarly inquiry. Interestingly, Medhātithi names only a few Dharmasāstra writers and does not quote any Smṛtikāra using the prefixes ‘bṛhat’ or ‘vṛddha’.

The chapter elucidates Medhātithi's peculiar views and acknowledges his authorship of the Smṛtiviveka, from which he cites verses in his Manubhāṣya. Based on available evidence, Medhātithi is believed to have flourished between 825 and 900 A. D.
