It is notably challenging to precisely ascertain when the dharmaśāstra works were first composed. However, evidence from the Nirukta, specifically sections III. 4-5, reveals ongoing controversies regarding inheritance and even quotes a śloka from an existing work on dharma. Bühler's analysis further illuminates the significance of such early verses. Gautama and Baudhāyana explicitly speak of dharmaśāstra, while Baudhāyana and Āpastamba list numerous sages who propounded dharma. Further attestations come from the Vartika of Kātyāyana, Jaimini, and Patañjali, who discusses dharmasūtrakāras. These references strongly suggest that dharmaśāstra works existed prior to Yāska, or at the very least, before 600 B.C., with dharmasūtras having achieved an authoritative status by the 2nd century B.C.

In structuring the comprehensive study of dharmaśāstra literature within this book, a specific methodological progression is followed. This begins with the dharmasūtras, advances to early metrical smṛtis such as those of Manu and Yājñavalkya, then proceeds to later versified smṛtis, and finally examines the extensive commentaries and digests, exemplified by works like the Mitākṣarā. Nevertheless, establishing the precise chronology of these early writers remains a significant challenge. It is important to note that the view proposed by Max Müller, suggesting that works in continuous śloka metre invariably followed sūtra compositions, is not considered acceptable in this study.
