While Yājñavalkya mentions Parāśara, the extant Parāśarasmṛti is probably a recast of an older smṛti. Evidence for an earlier tradition includes the Garuḍapurāṇa's chapter 107, which provides a summary of thirty-nine verses of the Parāśarasmṛti. Furthermore, it appears from Kauṭilya's writings that there was a work by Parāśara on politics, within which vyavahāra was also dealt with. The name Parāśara itself is indeed ancient.

The extant Parāśara Smṛti, structured into twelve chapters and comprising five hundred and ninety-two verses, exclusively deals with ācāra and prāyaścitta. This text mentions nineteen smṛti writers and frequently cites the views of Manu, even containing several identical verses. The age of this Parāśarasmṛti is generally estimated to be between 100 and 500 A.D. Beyond this, there is also a Bṛhat-Parāśara Saṁhitā, a distinctly later work with its own contents, spanning twelve chapters and three thousand verses. Other Parāśara-related works include a Vṛddha Parāśara, quoted by Aparārka, and a Jyotiḥ-Parāśara, cited by significant scholars such as Hemādri, Bhaṭṭoji, and in the Smṛti-candrikā.
