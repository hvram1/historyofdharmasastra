Baudhāyana is identified as a teacher of the Black Yajurveda, whose *kalpa* arrangement has been studied by Dr. Burnell and Dr. Caland. The Baudhāyanagṛhya presupposes the Baudhāyanadharmasūtra, with the Gṛhya (III. 9.6) naming Kanva Bodhāyana a *pravacanakāra* and Āpastamba a *sūtrakāra*. These figures, along with Hiraṇyakeśin, are also mentioned in the Dharmasūtra's *tarpaṇa* (II. 5. 27). Govindasvāmin later commented on Baudhāyana. The extant Baudhāyanadharmasūtra is incomplete; its fourth *praśna* is likely interpolated, and the third is also doubtful. Baudhāyana III.10 is taken from Gautama, and III.6 aligns closely with Viṣṇudharmasūtra 48. Dr. Jolly suggested a common source, though Viṣṇu likely borrows from Baudhāyana. Repetitions exist even in the first two *praśnas*, and numerous *sūtras* are identical with those of Āpastamba and Vasiṣṭha, despite Baudhāyana's style often departing from Pāṇinian standards and Āpastamba's.

Baudhāyana's form and structure frequently quote verses, even initially. It reveals knowledge of various *dharma* authors and their views, noting Asura Kapila as the originator of *āśramas*. Śabara, Kumārila, Viśvarūpa, and Medhātithi all refer to the Baudhāyana dharmasūtra. Bühler posited Baudhāyana as a southern teacher, citing five peculiar regional usages and views on Āryāvarta. Chronologically, Baudhāyana is placed later than Gautama, generally between 500-200 B.C., though Bühler’s arguments for an earlier dating than Āpastamba are unconvincing. The text also mentions several appellations of Gaṇeśa, like the Mānavagṛhya, and refers to the seven planets, Rāhu and Ketu.
