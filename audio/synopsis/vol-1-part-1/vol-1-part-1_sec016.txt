The revered sage Atri finds specific mention in the Manusmṛti, in chapter three, verse sixteen, establishing his ancient authority. His influence is notably embodied in the Ātreyadharmaśāstra, a text structured into nine adhyāyas, for which a summary of its comprehensive contents and its distinctive form is well-documented. Indeed, numerous works are styled as Atrismr̥ti, reflecting a broad textual tradition. Among these, a detailed summary of the Atrisamhitā, as printed by Jīvānanda, provides valuable insights into this lineage.

Significantly, Atri is cited as an authority particularly on the intricate subject of adoption, underscoring his practical contributions to dharma. Further attesting to the diversity of texts attributed to him are the Laghu Atri and Vr̥ddhātreyasmr̥ti. His authoritative stature is cemented by citations in other foundational works, such as a verse from Atri quoted in the Mahābhārata, specifically in Anuśāsana 65.1. Moreover, Aparārka extensively draws upon Atri's wisdom, quoting approximately a hundred verses attributed to him across a wide array of dharma-related topics. The enduring scholarly engagement with Atri’s legacy is highlighted by modern editions, including the Ātreya-smr̥ti edited by Aiyangar.
