Rishyashringa's contributions are frequently referenced across pivotal commentaries on Dharmaśāstra. The Mitākṣarā, Aparārka, and Smṛticandrikā, for instance, often cite his views on matters pertaining to ācāra, āśauca, and śrāddha. Among his specific precepts, one verse notably addresses the intricate subject of partition, and a distinct prose quotation from his work is preserved within the Smṛticandrikā.

His authority extends further, with Aparārka relying on his insights concerning both prāyaścitta and vyavahāra. Significantly, a particular verse, Yāj. II. 32, found in the Yājñavalkya Smṛti, is also ascribed to Rishyashringa, indicating a shared or attributed origin for this important dictum. Lastly, the Kalpataru refers to Rishyashringa's teachings specifically when elaborating upon the duties incumbent upon a wife.
