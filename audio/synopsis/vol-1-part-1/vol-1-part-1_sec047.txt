Pracetas is established as a notable *dharmaśāstra* writer within the vast tradition of Hindu law and custom. He finds specific mention by the revered authority Parāśara, a crucial detail highlighting his standing in one lineage of legal thought. However, it is equally significant to note his absence from the works attributed to Yājñavalkya, indicating potential variations in recognition or scope among different schools.

His work, as it has reached us, comprises various prose and verse quotations. These fragments are extensively cited by later, highly influential commentaries, most notably in the Mitākṣarā and in Apārarka's digest. Beyond these major sources, a limited number of distinct prose quotations attributed to Pracetas are also found embedded within Haradatta's commentary on Gautama, as well as in the Smṛticandrikā. Further adding to the complexity of identifying this authority, the tradition also refers to distinct figures such as Vṛddha Pracetas and Bṛhat Pracetas, suggesting either a lineage of writers or different recensions associated with the name Pracetas.
