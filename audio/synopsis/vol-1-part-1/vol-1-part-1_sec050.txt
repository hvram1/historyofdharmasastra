The textual presence of Yama is notable, beginning with direct quotations found in the Vasiṣṭha Dharma Sūtra, specifically at chapters eighteen and nineteen. Beyond these, various printed compilations in verse are ascribed to Yama, including the significant Bṛhad Yama, which comprises five chapters and one hundred and eighty-two verses. Commentators such as Viśvarūpa and others extensively cite Yama, preserving over three hundred of his verses spanning all topics, notably including vyavahāra, with some of these now traceable in printed editions.

Furthermore, Aparārka quotes a few prose passages attributed to Yama. His wisdom is also reflected in the Anuśāsanaparva, where specific gāthās of Yama are quoted in chapter one hundred and four. The chapter then delves into some of Yama's particular views concerning vyavahāra, drawing from sources like Bṛhad Yama and Laghu Yama. Significantly, Yama is profusely quoted across the various kāṇḍas of the monumental Kalpataru, underscoring his enduring authority in Dharmaśāstra.
