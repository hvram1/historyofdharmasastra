A sūtra work on ācāra and prāyaścitta is traditionally ascribed to Sumantu. This important figure is recognised by Paithināsi, who includes him among the thirty-six eminent expounders of Dharmaśāstra. His authority is further evidenced by extensive quotations found in significant commentaries by scholars such as Viśvarūpa, Kalpataru, and Aparārka. Interestingly, however, both Yājñavalkya and Parāśara do not enumerate Sumantu among their lists of dharma expounders. Beyond the Dharmaśāstra tradition, Sumantu finds mention in epic and puranic literature, appearing in the Mahābhārata and the Bhāgavata.

It appears that more than one work may be attributed to Sumantu. Aparārka, for instance, cites verses on dharma from a Sumantu text, which is distinctly noted as a different work from the sūtra mentioned earlier. Furthermore, the Sarasvatīvilāsa quotes numerous verses attributed to Sumantu, specifically focusing on aspects of vyavahāra. The fragments and references to Sumantu's contributions to Dharmaśāstra have been a subject of scholarly interest, leading to a notable reconstruction of his work by Prof. T. R. Chintamani.
