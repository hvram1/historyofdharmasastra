The influence of Kāśyapa in the early Dharmaśāstra tradition is clearly evident, with Baudhāyana, in his Dh. S. I. 11.20, citing a specific verse that encapsulates Kāśyapa's perspective. This citation, alongside other references, strongly suggests the existence of a distinct dharmasūtra attributed to Kāśyapa. Further evidence points to a Kāśyapa smṛti, composed in prose, which is preserved within the Deccan College Manuscripts. This smṛti, the contents of which address various aspects of dharma, further solidifies Kāśyapa's standing as an authority on Hindu law and custom.

Beyond these direct works, Kāśyapa's authority is further underscored by references in later commentaries and epic literature. Maskarin, for instance, quotes a verse attributed to Kāśyapa, indicating its recognized status. The Vanaparva, in sections 29.35-40, also preserves five gāthās, or verses, ascribed to Kāśyapa, highlighting the reach of his teachings into epic narratives. Significantly, Aparārka makes multiple references, mentioning 'Kāśyapa' thirteen times and 'Kashyapa' six times, suggesting a potential distinction or different traditions associated with these names. The Smṛticandrikā, furthermore, includes Kāśyapa among the eighteen principal upasmṛtis, affirming the enduring recognition of his contributions within the larger corpus of Dharmaśāstra literature.
