Devala frequently occurs in the Mahābhārata, and is notably referred to by Śaṅkarācārya as a figure relying on Sāṅkhya tenets. It is understood that a *dharmasūtra* attributed to Devala once existed, indicating his early significance in the tradition.

Leading commentaries and legal treatises, such as the Mitākṣarā, Kalpataru, and Smṛticandrikā, among other works, incorporate numerous quotations in verse ascribed to Devala. These verses extensively cover diverse aspects of dharma, encompassing ācāra, vyavahāra, and śrāddha. However, this collection of quoted material is generally considered to be a later compilation.

Similarly, the *Devalasmṛti*, a text of approximately ninety verses focused primarily on purifications, is also deemed a late work. The jurist Devala, whose contributions are thus evident across different textual strata, is believed to have flourished approximately at the same time as other prominent legal authorities like Bṛhaspati and Kātyāyana.
