The various editions of the Vāsiṣṭha-Dharmasūtra exhibit differing chapter counts. Kumārila’s assertion that this text was specially studied by Ṛgvedins prompts an examination, yet nothing inherently special within the Vāsiṣṭha-Dharmasūtra itself directly connects it to the Ṛgveda. Its style closely resembles that of Gautama, with many sūtras being identical to those found in both Gautama and Baudhāyana. The overall form of the Vāsiṣṭha-Dharmasūtra also bears a resemblance to the Baudhāyana-dharmasūtra. Medhātithi, Mitākṣarā, and Viśvarūpa frequently quote from almost all chapters of the extant Vāsiṣṭha, indicating its significant recognition. Vāsiṣṭha was aware of a broad range of literature and notably prohibits the learning of the language of Mlecchas, while also naming several authors on dharmaśāstra.

Vāsiṣṭha's references to the views of Manu are made with respect to a work almost identical to the present Manusmṛti, and these do not necessitate the postulation of a separate Mānavadharmaśūtra. Bühler's interpretation of Vas. Dh. S. 4.8 as a quotation from a Mānavadharmaśūtra is therefore contestable. Only in Vas. Dh. S. 12.16 and 19.37, where Manu is quoted, do we find no corresponding verses in the extant Manusmṛti. Conversely, over forty verses are entirely common to Vāsiṣṭha and the present Manusmṛti, leading to the conclusion that Vāsiṣṭha contains borrowings from the present Manusmṛti or its prototype in verse. Vas. Dh. S. 22 is notably identical to Gautama 19, while Dr. Jolly's view that Vas. Dh. S. 28. 10-15 and 18-22 are borrowed from Viṣṇudharmasūtra or its original, the Kāṭhakadharmasūtra, appears incorrect. Bühler's speculation on Vāsiṣṭha’s home being north of the Narmadā lacks definitive evidence. The earliest reference to Vasiṣṭha as a writer on dharma appears in Manu 8.140, placing the age of Vas. Dh. S. between 300 and 100 B.C.

Vāsiṣṭha’s views are ancient, particularly concerning secondary sons, the Dattaka son, niyoga, and remarriage. He enumerates only six forms of marriage, though his stance on matters such as adoption and documents differs from Gautama or Baudhāyana. The possibility that Vas. 18.4 (Rāmaka, or Romaka as a variant reading) contains a reference to the Romans has been considered. The author of the extant Vāsiṣṭha-Dharmasūtra appears to be eclectic, and the text itself seems to be a re-hash of the Āpastamba-dharmasūtra, Baudhāyana-dharmasūtra, and Manu. Passages quoted from or referred to in the Saṃhitās and Brāhmaṇas significantly outnumber those from the Ṛgveda, suggesting that Vāsiṣṭha-Dharmasūtra did not initially align itself with the Ṛgveda. Dr. Ram Gopal's remarks regarding the passages of Vāsiṣṭha and the three Gṛhya sūtras attached to the Ṛgveda are thus misleading. Other compilations include a Vṛddha-Vasiṣṭha, an early compilation, as well as a Bṛhad Vasiṣṭha and a Jyotir Vasiṣṭha. Yajñavāmin is known to have commented upon the Vāsiṣṭha-Dharmasūtra.
