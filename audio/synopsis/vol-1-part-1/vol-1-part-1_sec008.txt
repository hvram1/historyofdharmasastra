The Hiraṇyakeśidharmasūtra constitutes the twenty-sixth and twenty-seventh praśnas of the larger Hiraṇyakeśikalpa. Crucially, the work attributed to Hiraṇyakeśin can hardly be deemed an independent composition, given its extensive indebtedness to the Āpastambadharmasūtra. It borrows hundreds of sūtras verbatim from Āp., though it does incorporate a few distinct additions. Stylistically, Hiraṇyakeśin's readings are often perceived as smoother and more classical than those found in Āpastamba, and the arrangement of sūtras also exhibits some variations between the two texts.

Turning to the commentaries, Mahādeva's Ujjvalā on Hiraṇyakeśin shares a remarkable resemblance, being almost identical in content, to Haradatta's commentary on Āp. This close parallel led Bühler to suggest that Mahādeva's work likely borrows from Haradatta. However, it is also notable that Mahādeva's commentary, in certain instances, contains more elaborate matter than Haradatta's and occasionally presents differing interpretations.
