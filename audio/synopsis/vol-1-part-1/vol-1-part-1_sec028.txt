It is widely posited that an authoritative sūtra work by Śātātapa, primarily dedicated to the intricate aspects of *dharma*, particularly *prāyaścitta*, *śrāddha*, and *ācāra*, must have existed in antiquity. This original text's influence is evident, as numerous verses attributed to Śātātapa are extensively quoted and relied upon in seminal later commentaries and digests, most notably the *Mitākṣarā*. Such frequent citation underscores its early importance in the development of *dharmaśāstra* literature.

However, it is crucial to understand that this foundational sūtra work is likely distinct from the *smṛti* compilations also ascribed to Śātātapa. Scholarly examination indicates the presence of not just one, but several verse-based compilations bearing this name. Among these, specific designations like *Vṛddha Śātātapa* and *Bṛhat Śātātapa* have been identified, pointing to an evolving tradition where the teachings of Śātātapa were perhaps reinterpreted, expanded, or compiled in different forms over various periods, suggesting a complex textual history rather than a singular authorial voice.
