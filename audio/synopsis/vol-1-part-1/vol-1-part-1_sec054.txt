The text known as Shat-trimshan-mata is classified as a compilation, bearing a structural resemblance to works like the Caturviṁśati-mata. Its substantial influence within the Dharmaśāstra tradition is evident from the numerous passages quoted from it in significant later digests and commentaries, including the Kalpataru, the Mitākṣarā, the Smṛticandrikā, and by Aparārka. This widespread citation confirms its authority and recognition among subsequent scholars.

However, a critical absence of its mention in the works of earlier commentators such as Viśvarūpa and Medhātithi provides a crucial datum for its chronological placement. Based on this historical evidence, its period of composition is generally fixed between 790 and 909 A.D. A specific characteristic noted of the Shat-trimshan-mata is the lack of any quoted verses pertaining to the domain of vyavahāra, indicating either a thematic limitation in its original scope or a selective preservation in the citations that have come down to us.
