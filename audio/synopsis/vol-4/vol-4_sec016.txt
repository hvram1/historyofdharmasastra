This chapter meticulously presents a comprehensive list of *tīrthas*, offering an exhaustive enumeration of sacred geographical locations pivotal to Hindu religious practice. Drawing upon a vast corpus of ancient texts, including the Purāṇas, Dharmaśāstra treatises, and other allied literature, Kane provides a detailed compendium that serves as an invaluable resource for understanding the widespread significance of pilgrimage in Indian tradition. This compilation is not merely a gazetteer, but a scholarly endeavour to map the spiritual landscape as envisioned and prescribed by various textual authorities, reflecting centuries of devotional practice and textual commentary.

Following this extensive documentation, the chapter proceeds with crucial concluding remarks on *tīrthas*. These observations transcend a mere summary, delving into the foundational principles and the socio-religious import of *tīrtha-yātrā*. Kane's analysis here encompasses the *mahātmya* associated with specific sites, the prescribed rituals and observances for pilgrims, and the spiritual benefits accrued from visiting these holy places. Through these concluding thoughts, the chapter offers a synthesis of the overarching philosophy behind the institution of *tīrthas*, elucidating their enduring role within the broader framework of Dharmaśāstra and Hindu religious life.
