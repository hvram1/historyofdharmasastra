The chapter initiates its discussion with a comprehensive examination of Ekoddiṣṭa and various other śrāddhas, including the specific practices of hema-śrāddha. It then systematically outlines the elaborate procedure of sapiṇḍīkaraṇa, also frequently referred to as sapiṇḍana, an essential ritual for integrating the deceased ancestor into the collective lineage. A significant portion is dedicated to the ābhyudayikaśrāddha, where its specific occasions are meticulously detailed, alongside a thorough description of its ceremonial conduct.

Further expanding on ancestral rites, the text elucidates other pivotal observances such as the pratisaṃvatsarika śrāddha, performed annually, and the widely observed mahālayāśrāddha, dedicated to all ancestors. The mātāmahāśrāddha, specifically for maternal ancestors, also receives due attention. Complementing these śrāddhas are discussions on associated rituals, notably the symbolic act of letting loose of a bull and the meritorious practice of the gift of a cow. The chapter concludes by transitioning into Section IV, providing an overview of Tīrthayātrā, encompassing pilgrimages to various holy places.
