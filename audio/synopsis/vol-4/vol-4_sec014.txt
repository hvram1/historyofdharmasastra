The chapter on Gayā begins by exploring its significance, drawing substantially from the *Gayāmāhātmya* found in chapters 105–112 of the *Vāyupurāṇa*. Scholarly views regarding the dating of this *Gayāmāhātmya* are also discussed, providing a crucial historical context. Further, the antiquity of key locations such as *Viṣṇupada* and *Gayāsiras* is highlighted through their mention in the *Nirukta*.

A brief summary of the *Gayāmāhātmya*'s narrative is presented, leading to an identification of Gayā's most important spots. These include the *Phalgu* river, *Viṣṇupada*, and *Akṣayavaṭa*, which are central to the pilgrimage. The chapter also delineates the five *tīrthas* of Gayā. Central to the rituals performed here is the offering of *śrāddha* and *piṇḍas*, a practice deeply ingrained in Hindu custom. Finally, the chapter contextualizes Gayā's importance by listing it alongside other revered sites, such as the seven holy cities, the twelve *Jyotir-liṅgas*, and the four *Dhāmas*.
