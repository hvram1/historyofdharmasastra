This chapter commences with an exploration of Hindu eschatology, grounding its insights in an analysis and translation of Ṛgveda X. 14-18. It proceeds to meticulously detail the intricate funerary rites and observances prescribed upon the death of an āhitāgni, drawing extensively from the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa as well as the śrauta and gṛhya sūtras. Herein, the primary method of corporeal disposal, cremation, is thoroughly examined, alongside a discussion of burial practices.

Following the initial rites, the chapter illuminates subsequent post-mortem rituals, which include the ceremonial offering of water to the deceased. This is followed by the significant ritual of collecting the charred bones, which are then reverently cast into sacred waters. The performance of śānti rites, aimed at pacification, is also elucidated. Furthermore, the text addresses the historical and ritualistic aspects related to graves, and critically, defines the specific persons deemed competent to perform these multifaceted rites after death.
