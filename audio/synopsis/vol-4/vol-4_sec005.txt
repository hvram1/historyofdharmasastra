This chapter undertakes a systematic enumeration and elucidation of the diverse individual prāyaścittas. This involves not merely listing their specific nomenclature but also delving into the nuanced descriptions associated with each. The concept of prāyaścitta, central to Dharmaśāstra, serves as the mechanism for atonement and purification from various transgressions, thereby restoring an individual's spiritual standing within the framework of dharma.

These names represent the specific terms and classifications for different acts of prāyaścitta – or expiation – prescribed for various categories of sins or misdeeds. Following this nomenclature, the descriptions provide a detailed account of each prāyaścitta. Such descriptions typically delineate the specific rituals, austerities, observances, or charitable acts that constitute the expiation. They often specify the duration, method of performance, the qualifications of the performer, and the precise circumstances under which a particular prāyaścitta is deemed appropriate and effective. The comprehensive nature of this survey aims to cover all individual forms of such atonement, as outlined across the vast corpus of Smṛti and Purāṇic literature.
