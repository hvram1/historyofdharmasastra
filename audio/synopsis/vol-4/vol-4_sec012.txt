The Ganges holds a profoundly revered position, its significance meticulously eulogised across a wide array of ancient Indian texts. This includes mentions in the venerable Ṛgveda, extensive narratives within the epic Mahābhārata, and numerous accounts in the various purāṇas, all of which underscore its sacred status. This deep veneration extends to specific pilgrimage centres located along its banks.

Prominent among these sacred sites is the `Tristhalī`, a significant pilgrimage circuit encompassing Prayāga, Kāśī, and Gayā. Within this triad, Prayāga receives particularly exhaustive eulogy, highlighting its supreme spiritual importance in Hindu tradition. Consequently, various rituals are customarily performed there, such as tonsure, marking it as a crucial site for purification and spiritual rites.

The chapter also delves into the historical documentation of more extreme practices, notably the practice of suicide at Prayāga, which, in certain contexts, was regarded as a path to spiritual liberation. Furthermore, Prayāga itself is not a monolithic entity but encompasses a multitude of `sub-tīrthas`, or sacred spots, within its broader sacred geography. Among these, the revered `Akṣayavaṭa`, the imperishable banyan tree, stands out as a focal point of devotion.
