The profound consequences that arise from not undergoing prescribed expiations or `prāyaścitta` are meticulously examined. A historical account traces the evolving concepts of Heaven, Hells, and the various forms of torments described within the Dharmaśāstra tradition, illustrating the grim destinations awaiting those who fail to rectify their transgressions. Central to understanding these ramifications is the profound doctrine of `karmavipāka`, which meticulously details how the fruits of one's actions, both virtuous and unrighteous, mature and manifest, thereby determining one's future states and experiences.

This intricate system of justice underscores the imperative of penance. Furthermore, the crucial concept of `Śuddhi`, or purification, is illuminated, exploring its multifaceted role, including the specific purificatory rites associated with events such as birth and death. These rites, alongside general expiations, are deemed essential for maintaining ritual purity, the neglect of which bears significant spiritual and social implications within the Hindu framework.
