The chapter delves into Kurukṣetra, exploring its historical significance and alternative designations such as the northern Vedi of Prajāpati and Samantapañcaka. Within Kurukṣetra, specific tīrthas like Sarasvatī, Prthūdaka, and its seven forests are detailed. The narrative then shifts to Mathurā, recounting its history, including mentions of its twelve forests, Vrndāvana, Govardhana, and Gokula or Vraja.

Moving further, the text examines Purusottama tīrtha, more commonly known as Jagannātha. Peculiar features of this sacred site are highlighted, including its celebrated car festival, the extensive army of attendants, and five other important tīrthas within its environs. Notably, the presence of obscene sculptures on the walls of the Jagannātha temple is also remarked upon.

The overview continues with the Narmadā river, offering a eulogy of its sanctity and detailing its numerous sub-tīrthas. Following this is an account of the Godāvarī, describing the one hundred tīrthas mentioned in the Brahmapurāṇa, such as Tryambaka, Janasthāna, Nāsik, Govardhana, and Pañcavāṭī. The chapter also touches upon Kāñci before concluding with Pandharpur, emphasizing its chief temple of Vithobā, its large staff of priests, the temple dedicated to the devotee Puṇḍalika, and the distinctive Vārkaris.
