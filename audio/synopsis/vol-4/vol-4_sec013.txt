This chapter delves into the profound significance of Kāśī, exploring its ancient history and the extensive eulogy it has received across various texts. It notes that this venerable city, commonly known as Banaras, has been identified by no fewer than five distinct names, each carrying its own historical and cultural resonance. The discussion further elucidates the precise derivation of the words Kāśī and Vārāṇasī, tracing their etymological roots and semantic evolution.

Beyond its nomenclature, the chapter meticulously outlines Kāśī's sacred geography. Central to its spiritual identity are revered sites such as Viśveśvara and Maṇikarṇikā. It identifies the five essential *tīrthas* within Banaras, crucial for pilgrims seeking spiritual merit. The famed Pañcakrośī pilgrimage, a circumambulation of the sacred city, is also detailed, alongside various significant sub-*tīrthas* like Jñānavāpī. The chapter concludes by highlighting the extraordinary abundance of numerous *liṅgas* found throughout Banaras, attesting to its enduring sanctity and status as a pre-eminent centre of Hindu devotion.
