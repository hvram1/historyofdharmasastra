Holy places, encompassing mountains, rivers, and forests, have historically played an undeniably important part in shaping the culture and fostering the unity of India. The meaning of `tīrtha` itself demonstrates an interesting evolution, from its early usage in the `Ṛgveda` to its more developed understanding in later Sanskrit texts. Places come to be regarded as `tīrthas` based on various profound grounds, often associated with divine intervention, significant historical events, or the spiritual power emanating from revered ascetics.

The sheer number of recognized `tīrthas` across the subcontinent is notably large, and they are systematically classified into four primary categories: namely, `daiva`, `āsura`, `ārṣa`, and `mānuṣa`, each representing a distinct origin or association. Furthermore, the authoritative texts delineate who is ideally suited to undertake such a pilgrimage, or `tīrthayātrā`. Upon commencing this sacred journey, specific rites are prescribed to the pilgrim, most notably tonsure and a period of fasting, which serve to purify the individual and prepare them spiritually for the profound experience ahead.
