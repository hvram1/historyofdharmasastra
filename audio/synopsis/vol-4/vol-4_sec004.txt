This chapter from P.V. Kane's "History of Dharmaśāstra" meticulously examines the prescribed expiations for a range of transgressions. It distinguishes between grave sins, exemplified by the egregious act of *brāhmaṇa* murder, and those categorised as lesser offenses within the intricate framework of *dharmaśāstra*. For each category of sin, the text delineates the precise *prāyaścitta* and the specific procedure that must be meticulously followed for atonement.

The discussion further details the particular ritualistic components of these expiations, prominently mentioning practices such as tonsure. Significantly, the chapter also addresses situations where direct performance of these *prāyaścitta* might not be feasible, thereby outlining the permissible substitutes for expiations. This provides a comprehensive understanding of the mechanisms for purification and rectification of moral and ritual deviations as expounded in the authoritative texts.
