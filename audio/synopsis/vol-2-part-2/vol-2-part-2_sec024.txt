The discussion encompasses a range of minor Gṛhya and other rites, opening with an elucidation of the Pārvana sthalīpāka. It then proceeds to cover seasonal observances such as Caitrī, and details the Sītāyajña. The chapter further elaborates on the Śrāvaṇī and Sarpabali, providing context for the latter by noting the continuous tradition of serpent-worship evident from ancient times in Hindu practice.

Subsequently, the overview describes a festival held in honour of Indra, followed by an analysis of the Āśvayujī rites. The Āgrayana iṣṭi, a significant offering, is then examined, leading into the Āgrahāyaṇī observances. The Śūlagava, also designated as Īśānabali, receives due attention. The chapter concludes with a comprehensive study of Vāstupratiṣṭhā, outlining its forms and evolving significance from ancient practices to its modern iterations.
