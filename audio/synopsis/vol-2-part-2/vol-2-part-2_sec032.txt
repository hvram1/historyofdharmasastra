The Nirūḍhapaśubandha, or simply Paśubandha, constitutes an independent animal sacrifice, distinct from its role as a component within the Somayāga. This significant ritual is to be performed by an āhitāgni either every six months or annually, necessitating the presence of six officiating priests. The elaborate procedure commences with the selection of a specific tree for creating the yūpa, or sacrificial post, complete with its caṣāla, a head-piece. Concurrently, a vedi is prepared, upon which rests an uttaravedi, a raised platform featuring a square hole known as the nābhi. Animals are typically sacrificed to deities such as Indra-Agni, Sūrya, or Prajāpati.

The ritual then proceeds with eleven prayāja offerings, during which verses from Āprī hymns are employed, invoking the eleven specific prayāja deities. The Śāmitra fire plays a crucial role for roasting the omentum of the victim. Following the Hotṛ's recitation of the Adhrigu formula, the he-goat is choked or strangled to death. The adhvaryu subsequently extracts the omentum and offers it into the Āhavanīya fire, dedicating it to Indra-Agni, Sūrya, or Prajāpati. A Mārjana purification is then performed by the six priests, the sacrificer, and his wife. Portions of the victim's cut limbs are offered as paśu-puroḍāśa, while the heart is roasted on a pike over the śāmitra fire and presented as havis to Manotā. The priests and sacrificer partake of the ulā, composed of the remaining limbs of the victim.

Further offerings include the Upayāja and Anuyāja, utilizing parts of the entrails. The hotṛ then repeats the formula called Sūktavāka, and the Maitrāvaruṇa casts his staff into the fire. The ritual concludes with Patnī-saṁyājas, incorporating portions of the tail. The text also notes Kāmyāḥ Paśuvaḥ, animal sacrifices performed to fulfill various desires, and mentions the Ekādaśina, a specific group sacrifice involving eleven victims.
