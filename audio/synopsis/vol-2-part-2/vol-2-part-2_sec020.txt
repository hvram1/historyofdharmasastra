The daily ritual of Vaiśvadeva is understood by some authoritative texts to comprise three distinct *yajñas*: one dedicated to the gods, another to the *bhūtas*, and a third to the *pitṛs*. This chapter meticulously identifies the specific deities invoked during Vaiśvadeva, further noting that the performance of this ritual is generally prescribed to occur only once, specifically at noon. The text then proceeds to describe, in detail, the precise procedure for executing Vaiśvadeva, laying out the steps involved in its proper observance.

Beyond the practicalities of its performance, the chapter delves into the nuanced views regarding Vaiśvadeva's relationship with the more elaborate *śrāddha* ceremonies, exploring how these two significant rituals are differentiated or intertwined. A fundamental component elucidated is *Baliharaṇa*, also known as *bhūtayajña*, which forms an integral part of the daily offerings. Concluding this exposition, the text also addresses the daily *pitṛ-yajña*, clarifying its specific role and integration within the broader framework of these domestic rites.
