The chapter on *Devayajña* establishes that in the *sūtras*, *homa* constituted this practice, though by medieval times *homa* had receded, replaced by *devapūjā*. It critically examines whether images of gods were known in Vedic times, exploring the meaning of *Śiśnādeva* and phallic emblems at Mohenjo-daro pertinent to *Liṅga* worship. Evidence suggests images were known long before *Pāṇini*, leading to a discussion on whether temple erection and image worship were borrowed or indigenous. Details cover the substances used for images and the principal gods worshipped.

The ritual of image worship and entitlement to perform *devapūjā* are detailed. The text also covers *Śalagrāma* and other sacred stones, including *Pañcāyatanapūjā*. A comprehensive section explores the *Ten avatāras of Viṣṇu*, tracing the theory's germs to Vedic Literature. It examines when *Buddha* became an *avatāra* of *Viṣṇu* and the disappearance of Buddhism from India, noting the very meagre evidence for religious persecution. Beyond *Viṣṇu*, the chapter reviews *Śiva* worship, alongside that of *Gaṇeśa* and *Dattātreya*.

Earliest descriptions of *Viṣṇu* and *Śiva* worship are provided, detailing the *16 upacāras*. Specific aspects like flowers for different gods, *Tāmbūla*, *Namaskāras* to the sun, and the worship of *Durgā* are highlighted. The chapter concludes with an analysis of modern *devapūjā*.
