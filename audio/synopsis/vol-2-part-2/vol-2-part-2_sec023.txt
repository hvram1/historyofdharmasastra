Upākarma, the ritual marking the commencement of the annual session of Vedic studies, and its counterpart, utsarjana, which signifies the cessation from these studies, form the central theme. A notable divergence exists regarding the precise time for observing Upākarma. The text elaborates on the profound importance attached to the month of Śrāvaṇa and the Śrāvaṇa constellation, which are central to determining the appropriate period for this significant rite.

The historical procedure of Upākarma in ancient times is meticulously detailed, followed by an analysis of the specific constituents that define Upākarma in modern practice. It is also noted that a holiday typically follows the performance of Upākarma. Turning to utsarjana, further divergences are observed concerning the suitable times for its observance. Finally, a description of the contemporary performance of utsarjana rounds off the discussion.
