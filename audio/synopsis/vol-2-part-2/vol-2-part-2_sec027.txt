The chapter delves into the concept of *Vānaprastha*, the forest hermit, identifying *Vaikhānasa* as an ancient term for this stage and referencing an ancient work known as the *Vaikhānasa sūtra* or *śāstra*. It outlines the specific time for becoming a *vānaprastha* and enumerates the principal points connected with this life stage. Furthermore, an intricate classification of *vānaprasthas* is presented, as found in texts like the *Baudhāyana-dharma-sūtra* and other sources. Eligibility for this stage extended to members of all *varṇas* except *śūdras*, even including individuals from princely houses.

The text also discusses the practice of ending one's life. A *vānaprastha* afflicted by an incurable disease was permitted to embark on the *mahāprasthāna*, the great journey, until the body ultimately ceased to be. This act of terminating life, whether through *mahāprasthāna*, or by fire, water, or falling from a precipice, was historically allowed under specific conditions, with historical examples substantiating its occurrence. However, it is crucial to note that this practice was subsequently prohibited in the *Kali age*.

In terms of duties, most of those prescribed for *vānaprasthas* were largely congruent with those for *samnyāsins*. Consequently, the *vānaprastha* stage itself was ultimately forbidden in the *Kali age* by authoritative works such as the *Nāradiya-purāṇa* and other significant texts.
