The Cāturmāsyas comprise four significant seasonal sacrifices, each termed a parvan: namely, Vaiśvadeva, Varuṇapraghāsa, Sākamēdha, and Śunāsīrīya. These are performed on specific full moon days—Phālguna, Āṣāḍha, and Kārtika, with Śunāsīrīya occurring on the fifth full moon day from Sākamēdha, or a few days prior. Across these parvan days, specific observances are mandated, including shaving the head and face, refraining from using a cot, and strictly avoiding meat, honey, salt, and sexual intercourse. Five common offerings are integral to all Cāturmāsyas, which may be observed throughout life or for one year. The Vaiśvadeva-parva itself features three special offerings, alongside nine prayājas and nine anuyājas.

Following this, the Varuṇapraghāsa is performed during the rainy season, specifically outside the house. Two vedis are prepared, to the north and south, respectively managed by an adhvaryu and a pratiprasthātṛ. Its procedure largely mirrors that of Vaiśvadeva, but includes four special offerings in addition to the five common ones. A notable aspect of the Varuṇa-praghāsas is the requirement for the wife to declare or indicate any paramour she may have had, concluding with an avabhṛtha, or purificatory bath, in a river or similar body of water. The Sākamēdha, a two-day sacrifice, necessitates three iṣṭis and a mahā-havis comprising eight offerings to eight deities. This is then followed by a pitṛyajña, termed mahāpitṛyajña, conducted on a separate vedi, and the performance of a Traiyambaka homa offered to Rudra.

Finally, the Śunāsīriyaparvan entails three special offerings dedicated to Śunāsīrau, Vāyu, and Sūrya. Beyond these, the iṣṭi known as Āgrayaṇa, an offering of first fruits, is performed in the Śarad season on a full moon day. Other iṣṭis are also prescribed for various specific objects or purposes, such as the putreṣṭi for the desire of a son, or the Kārīriṣṭi performed for rain, among others.
