The Darśapūrṇamāsa—new moon and full moon sacrifices—begins by establishing its performance time. Initial rites encompass Anvārambhaṇīyā iṣṭi, śākhāharaṇa, barhirāharaṇa (kuśa grass bundles), idhmāharaṇa (fuel-sticks), and Sāyamdoha. Uparasatha day sees Sānnāyya and Brahmavaraṇa—choosing the brahmā priest. Praṇītā waters are prepared, preceding Nirvāpa (taking sacrificial material) and Prokṣaṇa (sprinkling) of materials and utensils. After the Haviṣkṛt call, rice is beaten and puroḍāśa (cake) baked. Vedi (altar) construction follows, with Patnīsaṁnahana (girding the sacrificer's wife) and Barhirāstaraṇa (strewing vedi with kuśas). Fifteen Sāmidhenī verses and Pravaramantra (invocation of fire) conclude this preparatory phase.

The central ritual progresses through the two āghāras (continuous ājya pouring), Hotrvaraṇa, five Prayājas offerings, Vasaṭkāra, and two Ājyabhāgas. This leads to the principal sacrifice of cake portions, including an offering to Agni sviṣṭakṛt. Thereafter, prāśitra is cut for the brahmā, Idā, and a thin purodāśa slice for the yajamāna. Hotṛ invokes Idā. Brahmā consumes prāśitra, hotṛ avāntaredā; all priests and the yajamāna partake of idā. The purodāśa for Agni is divided into four parts for the four priests, followed by Mārjana.

Concluding acts include cooking anvāhārya as fee for the four priests, followed by three anuyājas offerings and sūkṭavāka recitation. Prastara bunch and śākhā are consigned to fire, along with Śamyuvāka and throwing of paridhis. Patnīsaṁyājas, Phalīkaraṇahoma lead to SaṁsthāJapa by hotṛ and Saṁiṣṭayajus offerings. Finally, adhvaryu and brahmā depart. The yajamāna executes Viṣṇu strides, offers a final prayer, and the ceremony concludes with Piṇḍapitṛyajña.
