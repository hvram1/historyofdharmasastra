The chapter titled "Nṛyajña or Manuṣyayajña" meticulously examines the ancient Indian tradition pertaining to the honouring of guests. This profound practice, deeply embedded within the framework of Hindu *dharma*, traces its reverence for guests from the earliest Vedic texts, specifically the *Ṛgveda*, and maintains its significant emphasis through all subsequent periods of socio-religious development. A central point of inquiry in this exposition is the precise identification of an *atithi*, carefully delineating who qualifies for such special and prescribed honour.

Kane then proceeds to detail the various prescribed modes of showing respect and honour to these guests, illustrating the practical application of this particular *dharma*. Beyond the specific prescriptive rituals, the author underscores that the fundamental motive driving these comprehensive injunctions concerning guests was indeed a spirit of universal kindliness and benevolence. The chapter concludes by outlining the proper procedures and courtesies involved in a guest's departure, thereby completing the entire cycle of hospitality from initial reception to their respectful leave-taking.
