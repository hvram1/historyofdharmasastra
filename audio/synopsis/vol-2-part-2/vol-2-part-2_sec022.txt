The chapter on *Bhojana*, or taking meals, profoundly highlights the importance of food purity within Dharmaśāstra. It systematically outlines Vedic rules concerning eating direction, meal times, and appropriate vessels. Preliminaries like *ācamana* and *prāṇāhutis* were prescribed before *bhojana*, alongside guidelines for posture and quantity. Social etiquette is detailed, distinguishing *panktis* (rows of diners) and identifying *pānktipāvana brāhmaṇas*. Occasions such as eclipses mandated abstention, and rules governed what food should or should not be consumed, based on various grounds for prohibition.

The text then addresses specific dietary practices, including flesh-eating in Vedic times, the sacredness of the cow, *Pañcagavya*, and sacrificial offerings. Rules for the flesh of beasts, birds, and fishes are discussed, noting reasons for the decline of flesh-eating while acknowledging *Kṣatriyas* as traditional meat-eaters. Regulations for milk, its products, and various herbs and vegetables are presented. An exhaustive list identifies persons whose food might not be taken, noting significant fluctuations, especially for *brāhmaṇas*, and leniency for food prepared with ghee, oil, or milk. The evolution of rules regarding *śūdra* food for *brāhmaṇas* from *sūtra* times, and regulations for cooks and servers, are covered. Ancient liquor consumption is discussed; all intoxicants were forbidden to *brāhmaṇas* from *sūtra* times, though some were allowed for *kṣatriyas* and others, with various *madyas* identified. Post-*bhojana* practices like *Tāmbūla*, along with rules for sleeping, sexual intercourse between husband and wife, and the *Rajasvalā*, are detailed. Finally, the distribution of the king's duties across the day and night concludes the chapter's scope.
