The Smṛticandrikā of Devannabhatta stands as an extensive digest within the Dharmaśāstra tradition. Its printed text primarily addresses subjects such as saṁskāra, ācāra, vyavahāra, śrāddha, and āśauca, though the author is also known to have composed works on prāyaścitta. It is worth noting that the author's name appears with various spellings across different sources.

This comprehensive digest is distinguished by its profound reliance on and extensive quotations from numerous Smṛtikāras. For example, it cites over 600 verses from Kātyāyana alone, specifically pertaining to vyavahāra, meticulously naming the various authors and works referenced throughout its discourse. Devannabhatta, the erudite author, is identified as a southerner.

The work's contents further reveal specific points of divergence from the Mitākṣarā. Its probable period of composition is estimated to fall between 1150 and 1225 A. D. It is also crucial to recognise that several other distinct works also bear the title Smṛticandrikā, necessitating careful contextualisation.
