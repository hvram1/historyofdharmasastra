Pratāparudradeva, a pivotal king of the Gajapati dynasty in Orissa, is primarily known for his seminal work, the Sarasvatīvilāsa. This important text details the pedigree and history of his royal family, while also outlining its own purpose and extensive contents. The Sarasvatīvilāsa draws upon a wide array of prior works, frequently highlighting differences of opinion, notably between Bhāruci and Vijñāneśvara. It often cites explanations of certain sūtras and words from Viṣṇu, consistently presenting Bhāruci’s interpretations. Further scholarly references include works by Varadarāja and the Smṛticandrikā.

The composition of the Sarasvatīvilāsa is generally placed between 1497 and 1539 A.D., a dating that dismisses Foulke’s alternative theory regarding its chronology. Regarding the authorship of the Sarasvatīvilāsa itself, Dr. Gode and Mr. R. Subrahmanyam attribute it to Lolla Lakṣmīdhara. Pratāparudradeva, however, is definitively credited with the composition of another significant work, the Pratāpamārtaṇḍa, which is also known as the Prauḍhapratāpamārtaṇḍa.
