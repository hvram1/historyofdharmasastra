Govindānanda was a prolific author, known for his significant contributions to Dharmaśāstra literature. His independent works include the Dānakāumudī, Śuddhikaumudī, Śrāddhakaumudī, and Varṣakriyākaumudī. Beyond these, he also penned important commentaries, such as the Arthakaumudī on Śrīnivāsa's Śuddhīdīpikā, and the Tattvārthakaumudī on Śūlapāṇi's Prāyaścittaviveka. Son of Gaṇapatibhaṭṭa, Govindānanda's period of active literary output spanned approximately between 1500 and 1540 A. D.

However, scholarly analysis of Govindānanda's corpus reveals certain nuances. Notably, he exhibited a tendency towards imprecision when referencing the names of his own compositions. His intellectual engagement was not without contestation, evidenced by his protests against Dr. Bhaṭṭacharya concerning specific doctrinal or textual points. Furthermore, the authorship of the work titled Kriyākaumudī remains a subject requiring careful scrutiny. Historians of the period must also account for the two inaccurate statements made by Dr. Haraprasad Sastri, which often touch upon intricate details such as the calculation of years incorporating intercalary months, critical for chronological accuracy in Dharmaśāstra studies.
