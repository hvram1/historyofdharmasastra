Shūlapāṇi stands as an eminent writer on Dharmaśāstra, hailing from Bengal. His earliest known work is the Dīpakalīkā, a commentary on Yājñavalkya, in which he notably holds archaic views on inheritance. He is also credited with fourteen treatises, all ending in ‘viveka’, though it is important to note these are never mentioned as parts of his Smṛtiviveka. Among these, the Durgotsavaviveka is considered one of his latest works, while the Śrāddhaviveka remains his most famous contribution. Throughout his writings, he frequently names other authors and their works, providing valuable historical context.

His Sambandhaviveka was notably edited by Dr. J. B. Chaudhuri. The Dolayātrāviveka of Śūlapāṇi provides detailed instructions on the Dolayātrā, the festival of swinging, including when it is to be celebrated, with half of the work drawing heavily from the Skandapurāṇa. His Prāyaścittaviveka, printed with Govindānanda’s commentary Tattvārthakaumudī, delves into the etymology of the word ‘prāyaścitta’ and its extensive contents, quoting various works and authors. The relationship between Śūlapāṇi and Vācaspatimiśra is also a subject of note. Further significant works include his Tithiviveka, edited both by Prof. S. C. Banerji and Dr. J. B. Chaudhuri, and the Caturaṅgadīpikā, edited by Mr. Mano Mohan Ghosh in 1936. Numerous commentaries exist on his Śrāddhaviveka. Regarding his personal life, Śūlapāṇi offers scant information. In colophons, he is styled as Mahāmahopādhyāya and also Sāhuḍiyān, likely indicating he originated from a place called Sāhuḍi. He is generally believed to have flourished between 1365 and 1445 A.D.
