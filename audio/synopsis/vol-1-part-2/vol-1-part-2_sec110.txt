Nandapandita stands out as a voluminous writer deeply engaged with dharmaśāstra. His extensive literary contributions include a significant commentary on the Parāśarasmṛti, alongside another on Vijñāneśvara's seminal work, the Mitākṣarā. He further authored the Śrāddha-kalpalatā and the Śuddhicandrikā, which serves as a commentary on the Ṣaḍaśīti. His prolific output also encompasses the work styled Smṛtisindhu, with its summarized version known as Tattvamuktāvalī.

Among his thirteen known works, the Vaijayantī, a commentary on the Viṣṇudharmasūtra, was notably composed in 1623 A.D. However, it is his Dattakamīmāṁsā that earned him widespread recognition, standing as his most famous contribution. This pivotal work on adoption held such authority that it was regarded by British Indian courts and the Privy Council as a standard reference. Nandapandita's scholarship engaged both in agreements and disagreements with the doctrines presented in the Mitākṣarā, thoroughly setting out his own views within his writings.

While details of his personal history highlight his connection to various patrons, his overall literary activity is broadly placed between the years 1580 and 1630 A.D. This period saw the fruition of his immense intellectual efforts, significantly shaping the understanding of dharmaśāstra for generations.
