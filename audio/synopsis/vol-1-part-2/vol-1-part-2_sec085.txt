The chapter opens with an exploration of a significant writer on *vyavahāra*, Harihara, who is known to have flourished prior to 1300 A.D. A different Harihara, whose period is generally placed between 1150 and 1250 A.D., is recognised for having composed a commentary on the *Pāraskaragṛhyasūtra*. An intriguing scholarly question arises as to whether this *Pāraskaragṛhyasūtra* commentator was, in fact, a pupil of the revered Vijñāneśvara.

Further complicating the precise identification, historical records also make mention of a Harihara who authored a commentary on the *Āśaucadaśaka*. Despite the existence of several individuals named Harihara across different eras and specialisations, it is posited that the jurist Harihara, the aforementioned writer on *vyavahāra*, is most likely identical with the *bhāṣyakāra* of Pāraskara. This inherent challenge, arising from the numerous Hariharas known in the tradition, necessitates careful chronological and textual analysis in Dharmaśāstra studies.
