Haradatta is highly celebrated as a commentator within the scholarly tradition. His notable works include the Anākulā on the Āpastambagṛhya, the Anāvilā on the Āśvalāyanagṛhya, the Mitākṣarā on the Gautamadharmasūtra, the Ujjvalā on the Āpastambadharmasūtra, and a commentary on the Āpastambamantrapāṭha. Across these, he is particularly known for explaining grammatical peculiarities at great length.

He hailed from the southern regions and was a great devotee of Śiva, with a prevailing tradition suggesting that Rudradatta and Haradatta are identical figures. Haradatta offers interesting information on various subjects, most notably providing significant insights into a widow's right of succession.

Determining his precise date remains a difficult problem for scholars, though he is generally placed somewhere between 1100 and 1300 A.D. A crucial identification holds that Haradatta, the commentator of dharmaśāstra works, is identical with Haradatta, the author of Padamañjarī. Furthermore, the Haradattācārya mentioned in the Bhaviṣyottarapurāṇa and Śivarahasya is probably the same Haradattācārya cited in the Sarvadarśanasaṁgraha. Among other compositions, Hariharatāratamya and Caturvedatātpar yasaṁgraha are also works ascribed to Haradatta.
