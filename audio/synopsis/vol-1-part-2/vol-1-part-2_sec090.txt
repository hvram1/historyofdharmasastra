Shrīdatta Upādhyāya stands as one of the earliest *nibandhakāras* on *dharmaśāstra* originating from Mithilā. He flourished prominently between 1200 and 1300 A.D., with his most active period likely centred around 1275 to 1300 A.D. Notably, Shrīdatta offers very little information about himself or his family within his extensive works.

Among his significant contributions is the *Ācārādarśa*, which delves into various aspects of conduct and meticulously cites numerous earlier authors. His *Chandogāhnika* addresses principal topics pertinent to daily religious observances, drawing upon a range of established works. Further, Shrīdatta authored *Pitṛbhakti*, a treatise quoting several authorities, and the *Śrāddhakalpa*. The *Samayapradipa* is another notable work, outlining its specific contents.

A distinctive feature of Shrīdatta Upādhyāya's approach is his remarkably liberal stance, exemplified by his allowance of *Vaiśvadeva* rituals for *śūdras*. It is crucial to distinguish him from another Maithila writer, Śrīdattamiśra, who flourished towards the end of the fourteenth century.
