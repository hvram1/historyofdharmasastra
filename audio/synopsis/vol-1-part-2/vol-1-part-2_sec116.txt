The Lakṣmīvyākhyāna, more commonly known as Bālambhaṭṭi, stands as a significant commentary on the Mitākṣarā of Vijñāneśvara, distinguished by the array of works and authors it quotes. Notably, Bālambhaṭṭi espouses latitudinarian views concerning the rights of women, a characteristic that has shaped its estimate in numerous judicial decisions.

The authorship of Bālambhaṭṭi presents somewhat of an enigma. While introductory verses suggest an authoress named Lakṣmīdevī, the real author is identified as Bālakṛṣṇa, son of Vaidyanātha Pāyaguṇḍa, who was himself a pupil of Nāgojibhaṭṭa. Bālakṛṣṇa’s other contributions to scholarship include the Upākṛtatattva and Dharmaśāstrasaṁgraha. It is important to note that Vaidyanātha, the commentator on Alaṁkāra works, is a distinct personality from Vaidyanātha Pāyaguṇḍa, the father of Bālambhaṭṭa. This scholarly figure flourished between 1730 and 1820 A.D.
