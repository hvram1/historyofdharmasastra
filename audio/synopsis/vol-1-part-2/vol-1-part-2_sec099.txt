Rudradhara, a distinguished Maithila writer, made significant contributions to Dharmaśāstra literature during the medieval period, with his intellectual activity flourishing demonstrably between the years 1425 and 1460 of the Christian era. His work places him firmly within a rich tradition of scholarly output from the Mithila region.

Among his notable compositions are the *Śrāddhaviveka*, the *Śuddhiviveka*, the *Vratapaddhati*, and the *Varṣakṛtya*. Of these, the *Śrāddhaviveka* holds particular prominence as his most celebrated and influential work. It is widely recognized for its detailed and systematic exposition on the various intricacies and injunctions pertaining to śrāddha ceremonies, making it a definitive reference in its field. The collective body of his works thus demonstrates a comprehensive engagement with diverse aspects of dharma, spanning topics from ritual purity and specific vows to annual observances.
