The Vīramitrodaya, a vast digest composed by Mitramiśra, comprehensively covers all topics of dharmaśāstra. It is organised into distinct sections known as *prakāśas*. The printed *prakāśas* delve into a wide array of subjects, including Lakṣaṇa, āhnika, vyavahāra, tīrtha, pūja, saṁskāra, and rājanīti.

This work is renowned for being highly controversial. While it generally follows the tenets laid down by Vijñāneśvara, it also, at times, severely criticizes him. Despite these contentious points, the Vīramitrodaya holds a position of high authority in the Benares school of modern Hindu Law. Mitramiśra, the author, also penned a significant commentary on Yājñavalkyasmṛti.

Regarding its historical context, the work provides an account and the pedigree of his patron, Virasimha, from whom the very title ‘Vīramitrodaya’ derives its meaning. Mitramiśra’s literary activity predominantly occurred in the first half of the 17th century, culminating in this extensive compilation of twenty-two *prakāśas*, each dedicated to its specific subject-matter.
