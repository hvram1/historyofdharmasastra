The *Dandaviveka* of Vardhamāna, a pivotal text in the study of Hindu legal traditions, was first edited by Kamalākṛṣṇa Smṛtitīrtha. This work perhaps uniquely stands out as the most extensive extant Sanskrit treatise dedicated solely to the intricate domain of the law of crimes and punishments. Its scholarly rigour is evidenced by its frequent references, notably citing Halāyudha a substantial thirty-three times. The text itself commences with a profound and elaborate eulogy of *daṇḍa*, setting the philosophical framework for its detailed contents.

Vardhamāna composed this significant *Viveka* during the reign of King Bhairava of Mithilā, situating its creation within a specific historical and geographical context. In developing his extensive work, he meticulously consulted numerous other scholarly works and authors of his time, reflecting a deep engagement with antecedent legal thought. His intellectual lineage is further highlighted by his gurus, who included his elder brother Gaṇḍakamiśra, as well as the esteemed scholars Śaṅkara and Vācaspati. Vardhamāna himself was a prolific author, credited with the composition of no less than nine distinct works, and is generally understood to have flourished some decades prior to 1496 A.D.
