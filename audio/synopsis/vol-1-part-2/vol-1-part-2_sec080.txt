Aparārka authored a voluminous commentary on the Yājñavalkyasmṛti. Historically, he was a Śilāhāra prince, a fact corroborated by inscriptions of the Śilāhāras, which shed light on his family's history. Two kings, known by the names Aparārka or Aparāditya, belonged to one of the three branches of this Śilāhāra family, specifically the one that held sway in North Konkan. This significant commentary is generally dated to around 1125 A.D.

Aparārka's work is characterized by its extensive quotations from numerous authors and other works. A distinctive aspect of his scholarly approach, however, is his studious avoidance of explicitly naming his predecessors who had also authored similar digests. He held several peculiar views, which merit attention. Furthermore, there is strong evidence to suggest that Aparārka was acquainted with the Mitākṣarā. Despite his contributions, the Smṛticandrikā offers critical appraisal of Aparārka's positions, highlighting areas of contention.
