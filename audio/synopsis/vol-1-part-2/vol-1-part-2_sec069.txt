Bālarūpa's opinions find prominent citation across several significant Dharmaśāstra digests, notably the Smṛtisāra, the Vivādacandra, and also the Vivādacintāmaṇi. This widespread citation underscores his importance within the tradition. His scholarly contributions are recorded as extending to at least the significant domains of vyavahāra and Kāla.

Scholarly inquiry suggests that Bālaka and Bālarūpa are likely identical, indicating a single author behind these names. His chronological position is firmly established as being earlier than 1250 A.D., providing a crucial temporal marker for his work.

A particularly illuminating reference in the Vivādacandra speaks of 'the author of Bālarūpa', thereby strongly suggesting that Bālarūpa was not just an individual scholar, but rather a distinct textual work attributed to an author.
