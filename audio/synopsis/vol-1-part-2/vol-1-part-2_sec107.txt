Raghunandana is recognised as the last great writer of Bengal on Dharmaśāstra. His monumental encyclopaedia, the Smṛtitattva, is organised into twenty-eight distinct sections, within which he names and references over three hundred authors and their respective works. While these twenty-eight *tattvas* form the core of his major work, Raghunandana also composed other significant texts, including a commentary on the Dāyabhāga.

His personal history indicates he flourished between 1510 and 1580 A.D., a period during which certain Dharmaśāstra topics assumed particular importance, a subject explored in various papers on Raghunandana. Throughout his writings, he frequently quotes authors and works, revealing not only the scholarly milieu but also the prevailing attitudes of writers towards women and śūdras. Raghunandana demonstrated a profound mastery in the application of the Mīmāṁsāsūtras and Nyāyas, and internal evidence within his Tattvas allows for the establishment of their compositional order.
