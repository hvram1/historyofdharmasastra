This chapter examines the work known as "Prakāsha," an ancient treatise that addressed critical areas such as *vyavahāra*, *dāna*, and *śrāddha*, among others. While its precise nature as an independent digest remains doubtful, the prevailing scholarly view suggests it was most likely a commentary on the venerable *Yājñavalkyasmṛti*. The composition of this text is generally placed within the period spanning 1000 to 1100 A.D., situating it firmly in the later developments of Dharmaśāstra literature.

Significantly, various names appear to refer to this same body of work. The "Mahārṇava prakāśa," the "Smṛtimahārṇava," and simply "Mahārṇava," all terms frequently encountered in the extensive quotations by the revered Hemādri, are considered designations for this single literary endeavour. Consequently, it is highly probable that the work known simply as "Prakāśa" is indeed identical with the more elaborate "Smṛtimahārṇavaprakāśa," thereby consolidating its identity within the intricate landscape of Hindu legal and customary texts.
