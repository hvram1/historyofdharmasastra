Nīlakaṇṭhabhaṭṭa was the grandson of Nārāyaṇabhaṭṭa and the son of Śaṅkarabhaṭṭa, placing his personal history within a distinguished scholarly lineage. His principal work, the *Bhagavantabhāskara*, is a comprehensive treatise divided into twelve distinct *mayūkhās*, notably composed in honour of Bhagavantadeva, the Bundella chieftain. Nīlakaṇṭhabhaṭṭa also authored the *Vyavahāratattva*, further demonstrating his extensive contributions to Dharmaśāstra.

An estimate of his qualities as a writer highlights his systematic approach and clarity, which made his works highly influential. Indeed, his *Vyavahāramayūkha* holds paramount authority on Hindu Law within significant geographical areas, including Gujerat, Bombay Island, and North Konkan. Nīlakaṇṭhabhaṭṭa's literary activity spanned a productive period, flourishing prominently between the years 1610 and 1650. It is also noteworthy that there existed a significant divergence of views between Nīlakaṇṭhabhaṭṭa and his cousin, Kamalākara, on various interpretative matters within their scholarship.
