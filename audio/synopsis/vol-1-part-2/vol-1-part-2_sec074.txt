Bhavadevabhatta is recognized as the author of several significant dharmaśāstra works, including the Vyavahāratilaka and the Sambandhaviveka. He also composed the Karmānuṣṭhānapaddhati, alternatively known as Deśakarmapaddhati, which details the contents of ritual practices. Further contributions include the Prāyaścittanirūpana, and the Tautātitamatatilaka, a work specifically devoted to elucidating Pūrvamīmāṁsā doctrines. His scholarly influence is evident in Raghunandana’s commentaries, where Bhavadevabhaṭṭa’s views are both critically examined and frequently accepted.

Insights into Bhavadeva's personal history are offered through an inscription found at Bhuvaneśvara, which portrays him as a prodigious builder of temples and tanks. This historical context helps to explain the epithet 'Bālavalabhi-bhujaṅga' attributed to him. A review of his Prayaścitta prakaraṇa reveals the range of authors and works he quoted. Furthermore, his Śavasūtikaśauca-prakaraṇa, notably edited by Dr. R. C. Hazra, and his Śavasūtikaśauca-prakaraṇa, provide comprehensive details on the purification rituals concerning birth and death defilements.

Bhavadevabhatta flourished broadly between 1050 and 1150 A.D., a period that also saw interaction with figures like Pradīpa. It is important to note the existence of other authors on dharmaśāstra who also bore the name Bhavadeva, necessitating careful distinction in scholarship.
