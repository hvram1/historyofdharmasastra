The chapter dedicated to Govindārāja in P.V. Kane's monumental "History of Dharmaśāstra" commences by establishing his significant literary contributions. Govindārāja is recognized as the author of a comprehensive commentary on the Manusmṛti, a cornerstone text of Hindu legal tradition, and also penned an independent work titled Smṛtimañjarī, further demonstrating his scholarly breadth.

The text then delves into the personal history of Govindārāja, offering biographical insights while clarifying a crucial point of identification: he is distinctly not to be conflated with King Govindacandra of Benares. The narrative also illuminates the intellectual climate of the period by noting that Kullūka, another prominent commentator, frequently engaged in critical discourse regarding Govindārāja's interpretations.

A substantial portion of the chapter is allocated to detailing the contents of the Smṛtimañjarī, exploring its structure, themes, and contributions to Dharmaśāstra. Through careful analysis of available evidence, Kane precisely dates Govindārāja's period of activity, placing it securely between 1000 and 1110 A. D.
