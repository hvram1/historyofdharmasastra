Our primary understanding of Yogloka stems exclusively from the textual references found in the works of later, eminent legal commentators, specifically Jīmūtavāhana and Raghunandana. Jīmūtavāhana, in particular, rarely found himself in agreement with Yogloka's interpretations. Instead, his approach was largely critical, often characterising Yogloka's methodology as that of a mere logician, a taunt implying a perceived lack of true jurisprudential insight or traditional adherence.

From the surviving mentions, it is evident that Yogloka authored at least two distinct treatises, which are referred to as Bṛhad Yogloka and Svalpa Yogloka. These works, or at least parts thereof, demonstrably focused on critical aspects of Dharmaśāstra, specifically addressing vyavahāra, the principles of legal procedure and adjudication, and Kāla, the complex calculations and prescriptions concerning time for various religious and social observances.

Scholarly consensus, based on cross-referencing and contextual clues within these later works, places Yogloka's active period of scholarship and writing between approximately 950 and 1050 of the Common Era.
