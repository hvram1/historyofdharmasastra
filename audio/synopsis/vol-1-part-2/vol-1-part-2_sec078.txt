The *Kalpataru* of Lakṣmīdhara stands as an extensive work, profoundly influencing early Mithilā and Bengal writers. This monumental text is meticulously divided into fourteen *kāṇḍas*, whose intricate arrangement and specific contents have been thoroughly documented. Notably, eleven of these *kāṇḍas* were judiciously edited by Prof. K. V. Rangaswami Aiyangar. The personal history of Lakṣmīdhara is also detailed, establishing the composition of the *Kalpataru* between 1125 and 1145 A.D., within the realm of Govindacandra.

Regarding its textual relationships, the passages concerning *vyavahāra* in the *Kalpataru* exhibit striking resemblances, being more or less identical with those found in the *Mitākṣarā*. However, arguments advanced by Prof. Aiyangar suggesting that the *Mitākṣarā* knew or relied upon the *Kalpataru* are considered incorrect. The *Kalpataru* frequently interacts with foundational texts such as the *Manusmṛti* and *Yājñavalkyasmṛti*. While it is notable for its extensive size and wide scope, in terms of quality, the *Kalpataru* is generally regarded as inferior to the *Mitākṣarā* and several other prominent digests. Furthermore, specific passages from Devala, as cited in *Aparārka*, find parallels within the *Mokṣakāṇḍa* of the *Kalpataru*.
