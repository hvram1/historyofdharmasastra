Chapter eighty-one of P.V. Kane's 'History of Dharmaśāstra' is dedicated to a significant text identified as the 'Pradīpa'. This work stands out as an independent treatise, suggesting its unique contribution rather than serving as a commentary on, or a direct digest of, a specific prior authority. Its period of composition is estimated to fall between 1100 and 1150 C.E., positioning it within a pivotal phase of Dharmaśāstra literature's evolution.

The 'Pradīpa' is characterized by its broad engagement with various facets of Hindu law and custom. Its thematic scope notably encompasses *vyavahāra*, which addresses judicial proceedings and civil law; *śrāddha*, detailing the important ancestral rites; and *śuddhi*, which outlines the essential purification rituals. Furthermore, the text explores a range of other topics, indicative of its comprehensive approach to the intricate principles and practices of *dharma* during the early medieval period.
