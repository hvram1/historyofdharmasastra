The writers on dharmaśāstra were animated by deep-seated motives, diligently working to codify and interpret the foundational principles of dharma. Their vast body of work constitutes an unparalleled contribution to the development and preservation of Indian culture, shaping countless aspects of life from personal saṁskāras to the organisation of āśrama duties. While their extensive commentaries and treatises, referencing authoritative texts like the Manusmṛti or Yājñavalkya, occasionally exhibit certain defects or inconsistencies, the sheer scope and detail of their efforts were undeniably admirable. This monumental compilation of legal and customary principles, addressing even complex areas like prāyaścitta, remains an eminently useful resource for understanding the historical evolution of Hindu society and jurisprudence.

To conclude, a brief note is offered on the specific dharmaśāstra works and their prominent writers originating from Kāmārūpa, the historical region encompassing modern-day Assam, thereby highlighting a significant regional contribution to this vast intellectual tradition.
