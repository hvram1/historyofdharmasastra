The distinguished scholar Jitendriya holds a notable position in the lineage of Dharmaśāstra, primarily evidenced through his frequent citation by Jīmūtavāhana across his three seminal works. This repeated reference underscores Jitendriya's significant authority and influence on specific legal interpretations. One of Jitendriya's pivotal tenets concerned succession law, where he firmly held the view that the wives of a deceased person, regardless of whether they were living in a state of separation or in a joint family, were legally entitled to succeed to their husband's estate. This position highlights a particular understanding of women's proprietary rights within the complex framework of Hindu law.

An intriguing aspect of Jitendriya's legacy is the observation that no other early writer, apart to Jīmūtavāhana himself, appears to have cited him. This singular relationship suggests either a profound intellectual connection between the two, or perhaps a more circumscribed sphere of influence for Jitendriya's works during his contemporary period. Based on the chronological analysis derived from the context of his citations, Jitendriya is estimated to have flourished between 1000 and 1050 A.D., positioning him in a crucial era for the codification and interpretation of Hindu legal and customary traditions within the broader Dharmaśāstra discourse.
