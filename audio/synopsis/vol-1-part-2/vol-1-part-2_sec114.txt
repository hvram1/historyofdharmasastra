Anantadeva is primarily known for composing a vast digest titled Smṛtikaustubha, which encompasses various facets of dharma including saṁskāra, ācāra, rājadharma, dāna, utsarga, pratiṣṭhā, tithi, and samvatsara. Among these extensive sections, the Saṁskārakaustubha emerged as his most popular work, meticulously detailing its contents. A notable portion of this work, dedicated to adoption, is known as Dattakadidhiti, which comprehensively summarises important views on the subject. Other significant parts include the Abdadidhiti and the Rājadharmakaustubha.

It is pertinent to clarify that the name Smṛtikaustubha, as a general title, can be somewhat misleading, as it more precisely refers to the ‘Abdadidhiti’ or ‘Samvatsaradidhiti’. Beyond these major digests, Anantadeva also authored several prayogas. His literary range further extended to dramatic composition, evident in his play, ‘Kṛṣṇabhakticandrikā’.

Anantadeva's prolific work was undertaken at the command of his patron, Baz Bahadurcandra, whose family pedigree is also recorded. Anantadeva himself was the great-grandson of Ekanātha, a revered Marathi poet and saint, and his younger brother was Jīvadeva. His significant period of literary activity is generally established to be between the years 1645 and 1695.
