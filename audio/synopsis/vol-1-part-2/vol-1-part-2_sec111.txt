Kamalākarabhaṭṭa, the distinguished grandson of Nārāyaṇabhaṭṭa, was a prolific scholar who authored more than twenty-two works spanning several śāstras. Among these, a significant portion—about a dozen—are dedicated to dharmaśāstra, with the Nirṇayasindhu, Śūdrakamalākara, and Vivādātaṇḍava standing out as his most renowned contributions. Notably, he conceived of all his dharmaśāstra works as integral parts of a larger digest, which he intended to title Dharmatattva.

The scope of his writings is evident in the contents of works such as the Pūrtakamalākara, Śāntiratna, Vivādatāṇḍava, Śūdrakamalākara, and especially the Nirṇayasindhu. The Nirṇayasindhu itself is notable as one of his earliest compositions, completed in 1612 A.D., placing his primary literary activity between approximately 1610 and 1640 A.D.

Interestingly, Kamalākarabhaṭṭa occasionally presented viewpoints that diverged from those of his grandfather, Nārāyaṇabhaṭṭa, showcasing his independent scholarship. His works are characterized by many interesting observations, particularly evident in the detailed contents of the Nirṇayasindhu, which solidified his position as a preeminent authority in the field of dharmaśāstra.
