The first chapter of Kane's seminal 'History of Dharmaśāstra' meticulously delves into the concept of *Vrata* as it emerges within the textual landscape of the Ṛgveda. This foundational inquiry sets the stage for understanding one of the most pervasive elements of Hindu religious and social life, establishing its earliest contextual and semantic dimensions.

In the Ṛgvedic hymns, *Vrata* manifests with a rich semantic range, encompassing divine ordinances, cosmic laws, and the vows or practices undertaken by humans. It is often associated with the adherence to moral and ritual duties, particularly those observed under the watchful gaze of deities like Varuṇa, who upholds the universal order and whose *vrata* signifies his immutable cosmic law.

Kane's examination here critically analyses the various nuances of *Vrata* in its earliest attestations, distinguishing between its divine and human aspects, and tracing its nascent implications for the broader framework of dharma. This rigorous philological and contextual study reveals *Vrata* not merely as a simple vow, but as a complex socio-religious construct crucial for comprehending the evolution of Hindu legal and customary traditions.
