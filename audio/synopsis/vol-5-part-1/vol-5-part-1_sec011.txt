Chapter Eleven of P.V. Kane's authoritative 'History of Dharmaśāstra' is dedicated to a comprehensive scholarly examination of two profoundly significant Hindu observances: Makarasaṁkrānti and Mahāśivarātri. The chapter provides a meticulous exposition of these events, delving into their historical evolution, scriptural basis, and the diverse practices associated with them across various traditions.

For Makarasaṁkrānti, the treatise explores its astronomical foundations as the Sun's transit into the zodiacal sign of Makara, detailing the various rituals, charitable acts (dāna), and bathing rites (snāna) prescribed in dharmaśāstric literature. Similarly, Mahāśivarātri receives an equally thorough analysis, encompassing its mythological significance, the specific vratas and pūjās observed on this solemn night dedicated to Śiva, and the spiritual merits believed to accrue from its devout celebration. Through these detailed discussions, Kane elucidates how these observances are interpreted and codified by centuries of dharmaśāstra, becoming integral to Hindu socio-religious life.
