This chapter from P.V. Kane's "History of Dharmaśāstra" undertakes a scholarly examination of Navarātra or Durgotsava, two principal appellations for one of the most significant annual Hindu observances. It delves into the comprehensive framework surrounding this festival as prescribed within the Dharmaśāstras. The work meticulously explores the injunctions, ritualistic mandates, and traditional practices associated with this nine-night celebration, primarily dedicated to the worship of the Devi, or the Divine Mother.

The text systematically elucidates the distinct nuances implied by both "Navarātra," emphasizing the duration of nine nights, and "Durgotsava," highlighting its focus on the reverence of Goddess Durga. This analysis extends to the historical evolution and the varied regional interpretations of these observances, which, while sharing a common spiritual core, manifest in diverse forms across the subcontinent. The chapter therefore provides an authoritative overview of how this pivotal festival has been codified and practiced through centuries of Hindu tradition.
