Chapter Ten of P.V. Kane's monumental 'History of Dharmaśāstra' is dedicated to a meticulous exploration of two paramount Hindu festivals: Vijayādaśamī and Divālī. This scholarly overview delves into their profound significance within the fabric of Hindu socio-religious life, examining their multifaceted origins and historical evolution. The chapter meticulously traces the scriptural references and traditional practices that underpin these celebrations, from Vedic antecedents to later Dharmaśāstra commentaries.

A central focus remains on the specific rituals, observances, and customs that characterize these festivals. This includes a detailed account of the various pūjās, vratas, and ceremonies performed, exploring their prescribed methodologies and symbolic meanings. Kane elucidates how Vijayādaśamī, often associated with victory and the culmination of Navarātri, and Divālī, the festival of lights symbolizing prosperity and the triumph of good, are embedded within the larger framework of dharma. The discussion encompasses the social, ethical, and spiritual dimensions these festivals impart, illustrating their enduring role in shaping communal life and individual religious practice across diverse traditions of Bhārata.
