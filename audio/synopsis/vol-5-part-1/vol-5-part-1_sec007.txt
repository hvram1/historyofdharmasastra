The seventh chapter of P.V. Kane's magisterial 'History of Dharmaśāstra' embarks upon a scholarly exposition of several key *vratas*, which represent a fundamental category of religious observances within the Hindu tradition. This segment of the work meticulously examines a selection of these vows and ritual practices, delineating their prescribed forms and customary interpretations as found within Dharmaśāstra literature.

Central to this chapter's analysis is the *vrata* known as Nāgapañcamī, which receives a detailed treatment concerning its traditional context and associated observances. The discourse then meticulously addresses Manasāpūjā, delving into its specific injunctions and customary aspects. Further, the chapter encompasses a thorough discussion of Rakṣābandhana, exploring its established practices and traditional significance. Concluding this examination of prominent *vratas* is Kṛṣṇajanmāṣṭamī, which is subjected to comprehensive scrutiny regarding its observance and underlying principles. Through this focused survey, Kane elucidates the intricate framework surrounding these distinct socio-religious undertakings.
