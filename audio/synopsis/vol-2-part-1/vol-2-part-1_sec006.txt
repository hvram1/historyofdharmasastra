The concept and purpose of saṁskāras are central to Hindu custom, though discussions persist regarding their precise divisions and total number among smṛti writers. Despite this divergence, a generally agreed-upon list of saṁskāras emerges, applicable even to śūdras, with non-performance often entailing relatively easy expiation.

Garbhādhāna, documented from the Atharva-veda and detailed in the Bṛhadāraṇyaka upaniṣad and smṛtis, marks the initiation of this sequence, prompting inquiry into whether it serves the woman or the child. Common to all gṛhya rites, including saṁskāras, are the altar and homa, preceded by essential preliminary rites such as Gaṇapatipūjana, Puṇyāhavācana, Mātṛkāpūjana, and Nāndīśrāddha.

The progression continues with prenatal saṁskāras like Puṁsavana, Anavalobhana, Sīmantonnayana, Viṣṇubali, and Sosyantīkarma. Post-natally, Jātakarma is performed, incorporating homa and Medhājanana, followed by Nāmakaraṇa. This naming ceremony addresses historical naming practices, the prevalence of multiple names for an individual, and specific rules about names. Further significant saṁskāras encompass Karṇavedha, Niṣkramaṇa, Annaprāśana, Varṣavardhana, Cūḷa, and Vidyārambha, marking pivotal developmental stages.
