The chapter delineates the intricate framework of duties, disabilities, and privileges associated with the various *varṇas*. Central to this exposition are the *Brāhmaṇa*'s special prerogatives, encompassing the study and teaching of the *veda*, officiating at and performing sacrifices, and the unique activities of making and receiving gifts, with specific rules governing their acceptance. The text includes a high eulogy for *Brāhmaṇas* and undertakes a detailed enumeration and discussion of the distinct privileges they claimed.

While these were the ideal prescribed activities, the chapter also addresses situations of distress, where a *Brāhmaṇa* might undertake work typically designated for *kṣatriyas* and *vaiśyas*. This includes examining money-lending, agriculture, sale, and barter as permissible means of livelihood during such duress, alongside the practice of begging. In contrast to these elaborate provisions for *Brāhmaṇas*, the text then turns to the *śūdras*.

The *śūdras* are primarily assigned the special duty of serving the higher castes, with further divisions within their *varṇa* also explored. Crucially, the chapter itemizes the significant disabilities imposed upon *śūdras*. These include their explicit lack of authorization to study the *veda* or to perform *vedic* sacrifices with *vedic mantras*. Furthermore, they faced increased liability for punishment for certain offences and were barred from holding high offices, such as that of a judge, thereby presenting a stark contrast in their societal position.
