The vast purview of Dharmaśāstra addresses a myriad of subjects, commencing with a comprehensive exploration of dharma itself. This foundational concept is then meticulously divided, examining various classifications and types of dharma. A significant focus is placed upon the Dharmas common to all, identified as sādharana-dharma. These universal moral injunctions, such as truth, love, charity, and self-restraint, are presented as the immutable standard of moral values underpinning the entire system.

Beyond these ethical foundations, the texts delve into the profound concept of the four purus-ārthas, defining the ultimate goals of human existence and meticulously detailing their inherent gradation and interrelationship. Finally, the discourse situates these socio-legal and ethical frameworks geographically, meticulously outlining the limits of Āryāvarta and establishing the cultural and administrative relevance of Bharatavarṣa within which these principles are to be applied.
