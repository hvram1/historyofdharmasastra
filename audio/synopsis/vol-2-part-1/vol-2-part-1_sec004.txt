This chapter critically examines the historical and social phenomenon of untouchability. A close analysis of the earliest scriptures reveals that hardly any Vedic passage offers direct support for the establishment of such a rigid social stratification. However, with the advent of the *smṛtis*, we observe the emergence and codification of the concept of *Antyajas*. These foundational texts meticulously define various groups considered untouchable and lay down specific, often stringent, regulations governing their interaction with other sections of society.

The application of these rules extended to diverse facets of daily existence. For instance, detailed precepts governed the supposed ritual impurity arising from the shadow of untouchables, signifying a pervasive concern for ritual purity. Similarly, explicit injunctions were laid out regarding the presence of untouchables on public roads, indicating restrictions on their movement and access to communal spaces. Interestingly, the chapter also acknowledges scenarios, particularly in matters where immediate relief was required, suggesting that practical exigencies could, at times, influence or necessitate a deviation from, or a pragmatic interpretation of, these otherwise strict normative directives.
