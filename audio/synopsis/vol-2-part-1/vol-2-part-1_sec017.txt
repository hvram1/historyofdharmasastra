Chapter Seventeen addresses Āhnika and Āchāra, underscoring the profound importance of the gṛhastha stage. Householders are categorized into śālīna and yāyāvara, their extensive duties detailed across numerous Smṛtis and digests. Smṛtis typically divide the day into eight parts. The early morning dictates actions upon waking, including hymns, names of revered figures and cirajīvins, and observing auspicious sights. Calls of nature, bodily cleanliness (śauca), ācamana, and dantadhāvana – an ancient practice using specific twigs – then follow. Snāna, the ritual bath, is central, often requiring kuśas. Generally avoided at night, and preferring natural water, its procedure, including the use of clay, is outlined for purification and benefits, with Tarpaṇa as a constituent part.

Post-snāna, the householder dons specific attire and applies forehead marks like Ūrdhva-puṇḍra and Tripuṇḍra, noting sectarian condemnations. Sandhyā then precedes Homa, including the twice-daily Agni-hotra. Views diverge on its performance time, and guidelines cover the number of fires, initiation, and havis materials. Homa may be offered by oneself, or by a son, pupil, brother, sister's son, or similar relative; a wife or unmarried daughter may also offer it if the householder is unable. Japa of Vedic texts and contemplation of maṅgala objects conclude the first segment. Subsequent parts involve revising Vedic texts, gathering materials, and pursuing livelihood. The mid-day bath, followed by Tarpaṇa for gods, sages, and pitṛs, with a brief Tarpaṇa also prescribed, complete the outlined daily observances.
