This chapter commences with an examination of the *varṇa* system, noting both its high eulogy and its condemnation, alongside characteristic features of the modern caste system. It traces the history of the word *varṇa* itself, exploring the distinction between *Ārya* and *dāsa* or *dasyu*, and the role of the *viś* in the Ṛgveda. The discussion then moves to the position of the *Śūdra* in Vedic Literature and the internal relations among the three higher *varṇas* inter se. Early Vedic texts reveal various professions and crafts, including specific examples such as the *Rathakāra* and *Niśāda*, from which propositions regarding early social stratification are deducible. A comprehensive list of avocations culled from Vedic Literature further illuminates this period.

*Dharmaśāstra* writers subsequently delved into the ramifications of caste, attributing their origin largely to mixed unions and proposing two fundamental postulates concerning castes. This led to the classification of *Anuloma* and *pratiloma* castes, and the broader understanding of *jāti*. The texts meticulously detail the status of children born of mixed castes, the concept of *varṇasamkara*, and the dynamics of *jātyutkarṣa* and *jātyapakarsa*. The work also addresses the emergence of professional castes and guilds within this framework.

Finally, the chapter presents a historical overview, listing various castes mentioned in works from 500 B.C. to 1000 A.D., and notably highlights the revolt against the caste system as chronicled in the Mahābhārata, concluding with a glimpse into castes identified in medieval works.
