Chapter XIV delves into the intricate subject of the remarriage of widows. A crucial point of clarification emerges regarding the term ‘punarbhū’, which does not necessarily denote a remarried widow in all contexts. Nārada elaborates on various kinds of punarbhūs and svairiṇīs, while Baudhāyana and Kāśyapa further identify seven distinct types of punarbhū. Generally, the Smṛtis, with notable exceptions such as those of Vasiṣṭha, Nārada, and one or two others, prohibit the remarriage of widows.

Beyond direct widowhood, the chapter also addresses specific rules applicable to a wife whose husband remains unheard of for many years. In a later, transformative period, the Hindu Widow’s Remarriage Act of 1856 was enacted, a legislative response partly necessitated by the appalling number of child widows prevalent at the time. The discussion meticulously examines verses from the Ṛgveda and Atharvaveda, which are sometimes posited as references to the remarriage of widows.

A related but distinct concept, divorce, is highlighted as being largely unknown in both Vedic and Dharmaśāstra Literature. However, Kauṭilya’s works present a notable divergence by detailing provisions for divorce. This is then set against a comparative backdrop, considering divorce law as practiced in England and Roman Catholic countries.
