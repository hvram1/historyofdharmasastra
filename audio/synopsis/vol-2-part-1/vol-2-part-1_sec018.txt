The Mahāyajñas, understood as five daily observances or sacrifices, find their mention in venerable texts such as the Śatapatha Brāhmana and the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka. These are distinct from śrauta rites, differentiated in two significant ways. Reflecting ancient spiritual thought, the sentiments prompting these five yajñas in very remote times were profound. Over time, however, their purpose evolved, becoming explicitly stated as an atonement for the unintentional injury to life caused by one's daily activities.

In their prescribed order of performance, these five yajñas are enumerated as the brahmayajña, devayajña, bhūtayajña, pitṛyajña, and manusya-yajña. Among these, the brahmayajña holds particular significance. Its earliest detailed descriptions are also found in the Śatapatha Brāhmana and the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka, where its specific performance for followers of the Ṛgveda is meticulously outlined.
