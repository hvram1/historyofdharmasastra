Marriage is presented as the most important saṁskāra, with no textual evidence indicating a historically promiscuous society that preceded its establishment. The chapter elucidates the purposes of marriage, the qualifications of a desirable bridegroom, and the intricate rules for bride selection, which include an assessment of lakṣaṇas—both bāhya (visible) and ābhyantara (invisible or inferrible)—specific preference grounds, and sometimes the ceremonial choice from lumps of clay. Historically, brotherless maidens were not accepted, and medieval belief held that an unmarried woman would not attain heaven. Strict foundational restrictions included caste, gotra, pravara, and sāpinḍa relationship, the breach of which rendered a marriage null and void. While the age for men remained unfixed, that for girls varied, from about puberty in ancient sūtras to a later insistence on pre-puberty marriages in works like Yājñavalkyasmṛti, for reasons not clearly articulated.

The chapter details examples of inter-caste marriages in Vedic Literature, dharma and gṛhya sūtras, smṛtis, and inscriptions, with anuloma marriages permitted till about the 9th century A.D. Sāpinḍa relationship, as explained in the Mitākṣarā, is thoroughly examined, including its prohibitions, textual conflicts, and the specific issue of marriage with a maternal uncle's daughter, where usage sometimes narrowed the sāpinḍa rule. The meanings of 'viruddhasambandha', sāpinḍa for adopted sons, and its interpretation by Dāyabhāga and Raghunandana are clarified. Further, marriage between sagotras and sapravaras was forbidden; 'gotra' and 'pravara' are explained from Vedic works, highlighting their importance, divisions, and specific applications, with digest writers deeming such marriages void. The discussion extends to the authority to give a girl in marriage, historical "sale of girls" with the condemnation of monetary consideration, paternal power over children, and conflicting views on ownership. The text also covers auspicious times for marriage, various forms including rākṣasa, paiśāca, and svayaṁvara, the procedure as found in the Ṛgveda and gṛhya sūtras, detailing elements of the rite, and defining when marriage becomes complete and irrevocable, even when brought about by force or fraud.
