The chapter dedicated to the Veśyā offers a detailed scholarly exploration into the historical trajectory of this institution, firmly establishing its presence and evolution from Vedic times onwards. This meticulous tracing of its origins underscores the deep historical roots and the intricate societal role it played within the ancient Indian context.

Central to this discussion is an examination of the specific legal and social standing of concubines. Kane rigorously analyzes the various provisions and customary considerations that articulated their status, with a particular emphasis on their rights to maintenance. This analysis not only delineates the prescribed entitlements but also sheds crucial light on the frameworks through which a measure of economic and social security was, at times, extended to these women within the broader societal structure.
